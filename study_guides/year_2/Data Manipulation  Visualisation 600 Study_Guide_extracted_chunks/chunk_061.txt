4.1 Line Plots  
Matplotlib is well-suited for creating basic line plots to visualize trends and patterns in data. It provides a simple

4.2 Bar Charts 
A bar chart is a suitable representation when the objective is to illustrate the variation of a particular quantity 
across a discrete set of items. The image below shows step by step process of creating a bar graph using the 
Matplotlib library. Begin by importing the pyplot module from Matplotlib. Specify the data for the bar chart, where 
movies represent the titles of the movies, and num_oscars corresponds to the number of Academy Awards each 
movie received. Utilize plt.bar() to generate the bar chart, setting the left x-coordinates as range(len(movies)) and 
the heights as num_oscars. Enhance the chart by including a title using plt.title() and labeling the y-axis with 
plt.ylabel(). Label the x-axis with movie names at the centers of the bars using plt.xticks(). Finally, use plt.show() to 
present the completed bar chart. 

4.3 Scatter Plots 
A scatterplot is the right choice for visualizing the relationship between two paired sets of data. The image below 
shows the relationship between the number of friends your users have and the number of minutes they spend on 
the site every day. Begin by importing the pyplot module from Matplotlib. Specify the data for the scatter plot, 
where friends represent the number of friends, minutes represent the daily minutes spent on the site, and labels 
provide labels for each point. Utilize plt.scatter() to generate the scatter plot using the provided data. Iterate 
through the data points and use plt.annotate() to label each point with the corresponding label, slightly offsetting 
the labels for better visibility. Enhance the plot by including a title and labeling the x and y axes. Finally, use 
plt.show() to present the completed scatter plot.
