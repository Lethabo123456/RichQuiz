The edges connecting two vertices can be assigned a nonnegative real number, called the 
weight of the edge. If the graph represents a highway structure, the weight can represent the distance 
between two places or the travel time from one place to another. Such graphs are called weighted graphs.  
  
Let G be a weighted graph. Let u and v be two vertices in G, and let P be a path in G from u to v. The weight 
of the path P is the sum of the weights of all the edges on the path P, which is also called the weight of v 
from u via P.
