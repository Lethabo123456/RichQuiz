Working in teams also requires you to manage and address conflict. The basic types of 
conflict are emotional and cognitive. Emotional conflict stems from personal interactions, 
while cognitive conflicts are based on disagreements on matters of substantive value or 
impact on the project or organization. Resolution of cognitive conflict requires the team to 
focus on examining the premises, assumptions, observations, and expectations of the team 
members. Working through such problems can have the beneficial effect of strengthening the 
foundation of the analysis and the solution. Many conflict situations encompass both 
emotional and cognitive elements.
