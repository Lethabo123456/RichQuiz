The value of data ultimately lies in the decisions it enables. Powerful information-
analysis tools in areas such as OLAP and data mining, when incorporated into a data 
warehousing architecture, bring market conditions into sharper focus and help organizations 
deliver greater competitive value. OLAP provides top-down, query-driven data analysis; 
data mining pro- vides bottom-up, discovery-driven analysis. OLAP requires repetitive 
testing of user-originated theories; data mining requires no assumptions and instead identifies 
facts and conclusions based on patterns discovered. OLAP, or multidimensional analysis, 
requires a great deal of human ingenuity and interaction with the database to find information 
in the database. A user of a data-mining tool does not need to figure out what questions to 
ask; instead, the approach is, ‘here’s the data, tell me what interesting patterns emerge’. For
