Data Redundancy Revisited 
In Chapter 1, you learned that data redundancy leads to data anomalies, which can destroy the 
effectiveness of the database. You also learned that the relational database makes it possible to 
control data redundancies by using common attributes that are shared by tables, called foreign keys. 
The proper use of foreign keys is crucial to controlling data redundancy, although they do not totally 
eliminate the problem because the foreign key values can be repeated many times. However, the 
proper use of foreign keys minimizes data redundancies and the chances that destructive data 
anomalies will develop.
