5.5 The Logical Design 
 
Logical design is the second stage in the database design process. The logical design goal is to 
design an enterprise-wide database that is based on a specific data model but independent of 
physical-level details. Logical design requires that all objects in the conceptual model be mapped 
to the specific constructs used by the selected database model. For example, the logical design for 
a relational DBMS includes the specifications for the relations (tables), relationships, and 
constraints (in other words, domain definitions, data validations, and security views). The logical 
design is generally performed in four steps, which are listed in Table 9.6. 
 
Such steps, like most of the data-modelling process, are not necessarily performed sequentially, but 
in an iterative fashion. The following sections cover these steps in more detail.
