Natural Keys and Primary Keys 
The concept of a unique identifier is commonly encountered in the real world. A natural key or 
natural identifier is a real-world, generally accepted identifier used to distinguish—that is, 
uniquely identify—real-world objects. As its name implies, a natural key is familiar to end users 
and forms part of their day-to-day business vocabulary. Usually, if an entity has a natural identifier, 
a data modeler uses it as the primary key of the entity being modelled. Generally, most natural keys 
make acceptable primary key identifiers. The next section presents some basic guidelines for 
selecting primary keys.
