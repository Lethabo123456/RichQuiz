In the evolution of data models, some common characteristics have made them widely accepted: 
• 
A data model must show some degree of conceptual simplicity without compromising the 
semantic completeness of the database. A data model must represent the real world as closely 
as possible.  
• 
Representation of the real-world transformations (behaviour) must be in compliance with the 
consistency and integrity characteristics required by the intended use of the data model. 
Each new data model addresses the shortcomings of previous models. The network model replaced 
the hierarchical model because the former made it much easier to represent complex (many-to-many) 
relationships. In turn, the relational model offers several advantages over the hierarchical and 
network models through its simpler data representation, superior data independence, and easy-to-use
