Measures of Centre 
Measures of centre are statistical tools used to summarize a set of data by identifying the 
central point within that data set. The most used measures of centre are the mean, median, 
and mode. Each measure provides a different way of understanding what could be considered 
the "average" or "typical" value in the data. 

At the Ordinal Level: Median as the Preferred Measure 
1. Ordinal Data: This type of data represents categories with a meaningful order or 
ranking but without consistent intervals between categories. For example, satisfaction 
levels (like in your survey), class ranks, or any rating scales. 
2. Median: The median is a suitable measure for ordinal data because it identifies the 
middle value in a ranked order, without requiring arithmetic operations between data 
points. It splits the data into two equal halves. The median is less affected by extreme 
values (outliers) compared to the mean. 
• Example: In your employee happiness survey, the responses are ordinal (ratings 
from 1-5). To find the median, you first sort the data and then identify the middle 
value(s). If the number of data points is odd, the median is the middle number. If 
it’s even, it’s the average of the two middle numbers. 
3. Mean Not Suitable for Ordinal Data: The mean involves arithmetic operations like 
addition and division, which assume equal intervals between data points – a condition 
not met in ordinal data. As you rightly noted, the difference between ordinal values 
(like a 4 and a 2 in your survey) doesn't have a meaningful or consistent interpretation. 
4. Mode: It is the most frequently occurring value in the data set. The mode can be used 
for ordinal data and is especially useful when the data have one or more values that 
occur more frequently than others.
