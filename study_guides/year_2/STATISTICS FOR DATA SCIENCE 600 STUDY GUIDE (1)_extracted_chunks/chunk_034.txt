1.7 The four levels of data 
It is generally understood that a specific characteristic (feature/column) of structured data 
can be broken down into one of four levels of data. The levels are: 
• The nominal level 
• The ordinal level 
• The interval level 
• The ratio level 
The Nominal Level of Data 
Definition and Characteristics: 
• Basic Description: The nominal level of data is the most basic level, where data is 
categorized purely by names or labels without any inherent numerical value. 
• Qualitative Nature: This type of data is qualitative, meaning it describes 
characteristics or qualities rather than quantities. 
  
Examples and Explanation: 
Animal Species: 
• Description: Classification of animals (e.g., chimpanzee, lion) is nominal. 
• Additional Insight: A chimpanzee, for instance, can be further classified into the 
mammalian class. 
Parts of Speech: 
• Example: Words like "she" (pronoun) or "happiness" (noun) are classified on a 
nominal level. 
• Note: These labels do not imply any quantitative measurement. 
Mathematical Operations: 
• Limitations: You cannot perform quantitative operations (like addition or division) on 
nominal data. 
• Allowed Operations: 
o Equality: Determining if two items belong to the same category (e.g., both 
being mammals). 
o Set Membership: Identifying whether an item belongs to a certain category 
(e.g., a square is a rectangle).
