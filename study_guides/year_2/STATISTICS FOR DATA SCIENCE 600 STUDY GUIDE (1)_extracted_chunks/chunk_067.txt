Proportional Concepts 
Definition: 
• Proportionality is a mathematical concept where two quantities maintain a constant 
ratio. The lowercase alpha symbol (α) is often used to denote proportionality. 
Types of Proportionalities: 

7. Direct Proportionality: 
o Definition: Two variables are directly proportional if they increase or decrease 
together at the same rate. 
o Notation: y∝x or y=kx, where k is the constant of proportionality. 
o Example: If a car travels at a constant speed, the distance traveled is directly 
proportional to the time spent traveling. If the car moves at 50 km/h, in 2 
hours, it will cover 100 km (50 km/h * 2 h). 

8. Inverse Proportionality: 
o Definition: Two variables are inversely proportional if one increases while the 
other decreases at the same rate. 
o Notation: y ∝ x or y=xk . 
o Example: The time taken to complete a task is inversely proportional to the 
number of people working on it. If 2 workers take 5 hours to complete a task, 
10 workers would take only 1 hour (assuming efficiency remains constant). 
Real-World Examples: 
• Direct Proportionality: 
o Company Sales and Customers: If the number of customers doubles, sales also 
double, assuming each customer buys a similar amount (Sales ∝Customers). 
• Inverse Proportionality: 
o Gas Prices and Oil Availability: As the availability of oil decreases, gas prices 
increase (Gas Prices ∝ 1/Oil Availability).
