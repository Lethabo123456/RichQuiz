Interval Level Data 
Definition and Characteristics: 

• Interval level data represents a quantitative measurement scale where there is a 
meaningful difference between values. Unlike ordinal data, where ranking is possible 
but the difference between ranks isn't consistent or meaningful, interval data allows 
for the calculation of the difference between any two data points. 
• Key properties include the presence of a constant interval between values and the 
absence of a true zero point (meaning zero does not imply 'none' of the quantity). 
  
Examples: 
• Temperature: An excellent example of interval data. If the temperature is 100°F in 
Texas and 80°F in Istanbul, the difference of 20°F is meaningful and consistent. 
However, 0°F doesn't mean there is no temperature. 
• (Non-Example) Ordinal Data (e.g., Survey Ratings): In ordinal data, like a 1-5 survey 
rating, the difference between levels isn't consistent or meaningful. A jump from 2 to 
3 is not necessarily the same as from 4 to 5. 
Mathematical Operations: 
• Interval data allows operations like addition and subtraction. These operations are 
meaningful because of the consistent intervals between values. 
• Higher operations like multiplication and division are not appropriate as the scale lacks 
a true zero point. 

Measures of Centre: 
• Mean (Arithmetic Mean): The most accurate measure for interval data as it utilizes 
addition of all data points. 
• Median and Mode: These can also describe interval data but may not be as precise as 
the mean. 
Example of Using the Mean: 
• Consider temperature readings for a vaccine in a fridge: [31, 32, 32, 31, 28, 29, 31, 38, 
32, 31, 30, 29, 30, 31, 26]. 
• Calculating the mean and median (using Python) gives a close approximation of the 
central tendency of the data, around 31°F. This informs about the average condition 
inside the fridge.
