Measures of Centre at the Ratio Level 
Arithmetic Mean: 
• Still relevant and useful at the ratio level. 
Geometric Mean: 
• An additional measure of central tendency at this level. 
• Calculated as the nth root of the product of n values. 
• Example Calculation with Python: 
import numpy temps = [31, 32, 32, 31, 28, 29, 31, 38, 32, 31, 30, 29, 30, 31, 26] 
geometric_mean = numpy.prod(temps)(1.0/len(temps)) print(geometric_mean) # Output: 
Approx. 30.634  
• It's different from the arithmetic mean and can provide unique insights, especially in 
skewed distributions. 

Problems with the Ratio Level 
Restriction on Negative Values: 
Ratio level data is typically non-negative. This can be restrictive in certain contexts, like 
financial data where debt (negative values) is common. 
Impact on Analysis: 
The inability to accommodate negative values may lead to choosing the interval level for more 
flexible data analysis in certain scenarios. 

Data Interpretation and Structure 
Imposing Structure on Data: 
Statisticians sometimes impose structures (like means) on data levels where it might not 
traditionally apply, such as ordinal data. 
Assumptions in Analysis: 
It's crucial to recognize the level of data before analysis. Misinterpreting the data level can 
lead to incorrect conclusions. 
Summary 
Importance of Data Type in Analysis: 
Understanding the data type is crucial before any analysis. 
It dictates the methods and tools appropriate for the analysis. 
Key Questions to Ask About a New Dataset: 
Is the data organized or unorganized? 
Are the values quantitative or qualitative? 
What is the level of measurement of the data (nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio)? 
Impact on Data Analysis: 
The level of data affects the choice of graphs, the interpretation of these graphs, and the 
modeling techniques.
