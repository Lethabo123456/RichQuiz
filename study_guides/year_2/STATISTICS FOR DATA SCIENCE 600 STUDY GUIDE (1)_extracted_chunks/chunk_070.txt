Graphs are visual representations of relationships between two or more variables or sets of 
data. In this context, we will focus on Cartesian graphs, which are used to display data along 
two axes: the x-axis (horizontal) and the y-axis (vertical). 

Basic Structure of Cartesian Graphs 

• X-axis (Horizontal Axis): Often represents the independent variable. This is the 
variable that we change or control. 
• Y-axis (Vertical Axis): Typically represents the dependent variable. This is the variable 
that changes in response to the x-axis variable. 

Concept of Independent and Dependent Variables 
• Independent Variable (x): The cause or input; changes independently. 
• Dependent Variable (y): The effect or output; its value depends on changes in the 
independent variable. 
For example, in a graph showing the growth of a plant over time, 'time' is the independent 
variable (x-axis) and 'growth' is the dependent variable (y-axis). 
Notation and Plotting Points 
• Point Notation: Points on the graph are denoted as (x, y), where 'x' is the value on the 
x-axis, and 'y' is the value on the y-axis. 
Suppose we have two points, A and B, on a graph: 
o Point A: Let's say Point A is at (3, 4). This means it's 3 units along the x-axis (to 
the right) and 4 units up the y-axis. 
o Point B: If Point B is at (1, 2), it's 1 unit along the x-axis and 2 units up the y-
axis. 

Example 
Let's consider a simple example to illustrate these concepts. 
Scenario: A car’s journey is being plotted on a graph. The distance travelled (in miles) is the 
dependent variable (y-axis), and the time elapsed (in hours) is the independent variable (x-
axis). 
• At 1 hour (x = 1), the car has travelled 30 miles (y = 30). This point is represented as 
(1, 30). 
• At 2 hours (x = 2), the car has travelled 60 miles (y = 60). This point is represented as 
(2, 60). 
The graph would show a straight line indicating a constant speed, demonstrating the direct 
relationship between time and distance.
