document and share with others In a well-known Harvard Business Review paper called ‘The 
Knowledge Creating Company’ (from the November–December, 1991 issue), Ikujiro Nonaka 
describes four ways in which knowledge can be created.   
1 
When an individual learns directly from another individual, in an apprentice type 
relation- ship, tacit knowledge is created from tacit knowledge.   
2 
When two pieces of explicit knowledge are combined. For example, a website mash-
up could be considered an example of this type of new knowledge. (Mash-ups were described 
in Chapter 6 as the combining of information from two or more web pages into one web page.)   
3 
When an expert writes a book teaching others, explicit knowledge is being created from 
tacit knowledge.   
4 
When someone reads that book and (eventually) becomes an expert themselves, tacit 
knowledge has been created by explicit knowledge.
