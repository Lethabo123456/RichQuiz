Programmed versus Non-Programmed Decisions  
In the choice stage, various factors influence the decision maker’s selection of a solution. One 
such factor is whether the decision can be programmed. Programmed decisions are made 
using a rule, procedure or quantitative method. For example, to say that inventory should be 
ordered when inventory levels drop to 100 units is a programmed decision because it adheres 
to a rule. Programmed decisions are easy to computerize using traditional information systems.
