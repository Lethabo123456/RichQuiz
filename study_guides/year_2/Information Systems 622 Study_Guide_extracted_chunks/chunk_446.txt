Crime-fighting procedures usually require additional controls on the information system. 
Before designing and implementing controls, organizations must consider the types of 
computer-related crime that might occur, the consequences of these crimes, and the cost and 
complexity of needed controls. In most cases, organizations conclude that the trade-off between 
crime and the additional cost and complexity weighs in favour of better system controls. Having 
knowledge of some of the methods used to commit crime is also helpful in preventing, detecting 
and developing systems resistant to computer crime. Some companies actually hire former 
criminals to thwart other criminals.
