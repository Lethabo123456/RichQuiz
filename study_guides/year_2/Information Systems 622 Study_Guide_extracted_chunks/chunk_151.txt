Because knowledge can be critical in maintaining a competitive advantage, businesses should 
be careful in how they share it. Although they want important decision makers inside and 
outside the organization to have complete and easy access to knowledge, they also need to 
protect knowledge from competitors and others who shouldn’t see it. As a result, many 
businesses use patents, copyrights, trade secrets, Internet firewalls and other measures to keep 
prying eyes from seeing important knowledge that is often expensive and hard to create. In 
addition to using information systems and collaborative software tools to share knowledge, 
some organizations use non-technical approaches. These include corporate retreats and 
gatherings, sporting events, informal knowledge worker lounges or meeting places, kitchen 
facilities, day-care centres and comfortable workout centres. Using a knowledge management
