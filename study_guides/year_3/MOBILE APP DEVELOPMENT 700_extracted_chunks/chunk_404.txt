There are several steps to getting a debug key for a maps application: 

1. Get the SHA1 fingerprint for your project: Select View > Tool Windows > Gradle. A panel titled 
Gradle will open on the right side of the editor. 
2. Expand the project until you see :app. Expand that. Expand Tasks. Expand android. Double-click 
signingReport (on the left side of Figure 7.8. The Run panel will open at the bottom of Android 
Studio. 
3. Locate SHA1: in the Config: debug portion of the report (If there is no report, you may have to 
click the run button on the top left of the panel.) Copy the long hexadecimal number. (Be careful 
to only copy the key and not the SHA1: header.) Note that the key will look similar to this: 
72:27:E9:A6:AF:85:77:C2:4C:F2:22:45:72:98:1B:2C:50:F4:07:B1. 
4. Google now requires new users of the Maps platform to set up a billing account to gain access 
to it. The billing account requires a credit card. What we do with maps in this book falls into the 
free access use category, but they still require the billing account to get a key. If you are 
interested in continuing, you need to do this to get maps to work. Since procedures change 
frequently, please refer to the following to set up the account and get a map key: 
https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/android-sdk/intro. You will need the 
SHA1 fingerprint you generated previously to obtain the key. 
5. If you have a key, return to Android Studio and open the AndroidManifest.xml file. Just before 
the </application> tag at the bottom of the file, enter the following:

<meta-data android:name="com.google.android.maps.v2.API_KEY" 
android:value="YourKeyGoesHere" /> 
 
6. You also must add the location of the library for the map objects in the manifest. Enter the 
following after the line you added previously and before the </application> tag: 
 
<uses-library android:name="org.apache.http.legacy" android:require="false" /> 
 
The process of getting a production map key so that you can release the app to other individuals is 
essentially the same. However, you have to create a new keystore and get the SHA1 fingerprint from 
that keystore (see http://developer.android.com/tools/publishing/app-signing.html).
