You can use the dropdown button to select a new size, or you can click 
the oval to the right of the dropdown button to create your own named dimension resource. Click on 
the oval. The Resources window is displayed. All the resources created when the project was created 
are listed. Click the Add new resource dropdown on the upper right of the window and select New 
Dimension Value … (which may be the only choice). In the window that opens, enter 
message_text_size as the Resource name and 24sp as the Resource value. Click OK until you have 
closed these two windows. The size of Hello World should be increased. If you examine the res folder 
in the Project panel at the left of the IDE, you should now have a dimens.xml file. Open the dimens.xml 
file to see the dimension you created. 

Valid dimensions for Android include px (pixels), in (inches), mm (millimeters), pt (points), dp/dip 
(density-independent pixels), and sp (scale-independent pixels). It is generally recommended that dp 
be used for most dimensions and sp be used for specifying font sizes. These two units of measure are 
relative to screen density. They help keep your UI consistent among different devices. The reason that 
the sp unit is recommended for fonts is because it also scales to the user’s preference in font size. 
Close the dimens.xml file and click back to the activity_main.xml tab. Switch from Design view to the 
Text view and examine the XML changes to the TextView element. 

Switch back to the Design view, and locate the TextView widget in the Common folder in the Palette. 
Click and drag it to the layout, position it somewhere below the Hello World TextView, and drop it. 
Notice the round circles on all four sides of the TextView. These circles are used to position the widget 
relative to other widgets. Before you do that, switch to Text view and examine the new TextView’s 
XML. Notice that it has two attributes for the absoluteX and Y positions. These are design time 
positions. You should also notice that the TextView is underlined in red. Hover over the TextView, and 
you will see a pop-up message that says that the TextView will position itself at 0, 0 (the top left of the 
screen) at runtime if you don’t change the constraints. Switch back to the Design view. You will find 
that positioning is easier if you zoom in. At the top right of the Design panel are – and + buttons for 
zooming in and out. Zoom so only the new and old TextViews are visible on the screen. Click and hold 
the top circle of the new TextView. Start dragging toward the Hello World TextView. You should see a 
green arrow. Drag the arrowhead to the bottom circle on the Hello World TextView and release. The 
new TextView will move up and show a dimension of 8 between the two TextViews; 8dp is the default 
distance between widgets. Next, click on the left circle and drag the arrowhead to the left side of the 
screen. Again, the widget will move to 8dp from the edge. Click the Text view (Listing 3.4). A number 
of changes have been made to the XML (and some of your dimensions may be different).
