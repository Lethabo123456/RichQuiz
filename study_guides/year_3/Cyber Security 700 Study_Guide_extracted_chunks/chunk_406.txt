5.3 Firewalls 
 
The firewall is an important complement to host-based security services such as intrusion detection 
systems. Typically, a firewall is inserted between the premises network and the Internet to establish 
a controlled link and to erect an outer security wall or perimeter. The aim of this perimeter is to protect 
the premises network from Internet-based attacks and to provide a single choke point where security 
and auditing are imposed. Firewalls are also deployed internally in an enterprise network to segregate 
portions of the network. 
A firewall provides an additional layer of defense, insulating internal systems from external networks 
or other parts of the internal network. This follows the classic military doctrine of “defense in depth,” 
which is applicable to IT security. 

Firewall Characteristics 

“Network Firewalls” [BELL94] lists the following design goals for a firewall: 
 All traffic from inside to outside, and vice versa, must pass through the firewall. This is 
achieved by physically blocking all access to the local network except via the firewall. Various 
configurations are possible, as explained later in this chapter. 
 Only authorized traffic, as defined by the local security policy, is allowed to pass. Various types 
of firewalls are used, and they implement various types of security policies, as explained later 
in this chapter. 
 The firewall itself is immune to penetration. This implies the use of a hardened system with a 
secured operating system. Trusted computer systems are suitable for hosting a firewall and 
often required in government applications.
