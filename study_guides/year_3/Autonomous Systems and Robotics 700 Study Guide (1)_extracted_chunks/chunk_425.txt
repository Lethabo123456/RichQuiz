What the Robot Learns 
This model learns to trust different sensors in different situations: 
• In darkness → rely more on LIDAR or ultrasonic 
• In cluttered environments → use IMU to stabilize decisions 
• On open roads → trust camera vision for direction 

It builds a robust, generalizable decision policy across varied environments by learning the best 
combination of sensor inputs. 
Neural networks are a foundational tool in modern robotics, enabling machines to learn from 
data, adapt to new environments, and perform complex tasks that are difficult or impossible to 
program manually. Inspired by the structure of the human brain, neural networks consist of layers 
of interconnected neurons that transform inputs through learnable weights and non-linear 
activation functions. 

In robotics, neural networks are particularly valuable for handling high-dimensional, noisy, and 
uncertain sensory data. They are used extensively in perception, control, and decision-making 
tasks, forming the basis for learning-based approaches to autonomy.

Key Applications in Robotics 
1. Perception and Sensor Processing 
Neural networks, especially convolutional neural networks (CNNs), are used to extract 
spatial features from camera images, LIDAR scans, and other high-dimensional sensor 
inputs. This allows robots to recognize objects, identify obstacles, classify terrain, and 
navigate their environment. 
2. Sequential and Temporal Reasoning 
Recurrent neural networks (RNNs), including variants like LSTMs and GRUs, are 
employed when robots must interpret time-series data. For instance, they help in 
trajectory prediction, interpreting motion from IMU sensors, or planning based on past 
events. 
3. Sensor Fusion 
Real-world robots integrate data from multiple sources (e.g., camera, LIDAR, ultrasonic, 
IMU) to improve robustness and reliability. Multi-modal deep learning architectures 
enable these diverse sensor modalities to be fused intelligently, allowing the robot to 
maintain awareness even when some sensors are degraded. 
4. Control and Decision Making 
Neural networks can approximate control policies directly, mapping sensory input to 
motor commands. In reinforcement learning contexts, such as with Deep Q-Networks 
(DQNs), neural networks serve as function approximators for action-value functions, 
enabling robots to learn optimal behaviours through interaction with their environment.
