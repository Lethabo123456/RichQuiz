6.1.3.2 xxDeep Q-Networks (DQN): Function Approximation for Large State 
Spaces 
Q-learning performs well for small, discrete environments, but becomes intractable when the 
state space is high-dimensional or continuous, such as in robotic perception (camera images, 
LIDAR data) or real-time sensor feedback. 
To overcome this limitation, Deep Q-Networks (DQN) were introduced by Mnih et al. (2015) in 
their landmark DeepMind paper. DQN replaces the Q-table with a deep neural network that 
approximates the Q-function: 
 
Key Innovations in DQN 

1. Experience Replay: 
Transitions are stored in a replay buffer and sampled randomly for training, breaking the 
temporal correlation between experiences and improving convergence. 
2. Target Network: 
A second, periodically updated network is used to generate the target Q-values to 
stabilize training.

Use in Robotic Learning 
DQN is capable of handling: 
• Sensor-based control (e.g., camera, GPS, sonar) 
• Autonomous exploration and path planning in unstructured environments 
• Energy-aware decision-making with continuous state inputs 
 
Example:  
Improvement of previous Q-learning examples to deep Q-learning: 
We started with a basic delivery robot battery system: 
• States: 3 discrete levels (Low, Medium, High) 
• Actions: 2 choices (Deliver, Recharge) 
• Goal: Maximize delivery rewards while avoiding battery depletion 
 
This worked well with Q-Learning because: 
• Small state space (3 states) 
• Simple transitions (recharge goes up one level, deliver goes down one level) 
• Easy to store in a 3×2 Q-table
