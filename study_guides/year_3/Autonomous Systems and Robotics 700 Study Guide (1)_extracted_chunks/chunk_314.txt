5.9.2 Domain-Specific Simulation Requirements 
Different application domains in multi-agent robotics require specialized simulation capabilities 
that address the unique physical, environmental, and operational characteristics of specific 
deployment scenarios. These domain-specific requirements often drive the development of 
custom simulation platforms that can accurately model the relevant aspects of particular 
application environments while providing the performance and scalability needed for systematic 
research and development. 
Aerial swarm simulations require sophisticated aerodynamics modeling, wind field simulation, 
and three-dimensional collision avoidance capabilities that accurately represent the challenges 
of coordinating multiple unmanned aerial vehicles in complex airspace environments. These 
simulations must model not only the flight dynamics of individual vehicles but also the 
aerodynamic interactions between vehicles flying in close formation and the effects of 
atmospheric disturbances on swarm coordination. 
Marine robotics applications demand simulation environments that can model underwater 
dynamics, communication limitations imposed by the aquatic environment, and the unique 
navigation challenges associated with three-dimensional underwater environments where GPS is 
unavailable and acoustic communication has limited range and bandwidth. These simulations 
often require integration with oceanographic models that represent current patterns, 
temperature gradients, and other environmental factors that affect robot operations. 

Industrial automation scenarios require simulation environments that can model complex factory 
layouts, conveyor systems, robotic manipulators, and the safety constraints that are critical in 
human-robot collaborative environments. These simulations must accurately represent the 
timing constraints, safety protocols, and coordination requirements that characterize modern 
automated manufacturing systems.
