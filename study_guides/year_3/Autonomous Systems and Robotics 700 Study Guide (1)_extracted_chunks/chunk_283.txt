5.4  Swarm Intelligence Algorithms 
Swarm intelligence algorithms represent a fascinating intersection of biological inspiration and 
computational optimization, drawing from the collective behaviours observed in natural systems 
such as ant colonies, bee swarms, and bird flocks to create powerful distributed optimization and 
coordination mechanisms for artificial multi-agent systems. These algorithms achieve remarkable 
collective intelligence through the application of simple local rules that, when executed by many 
agents simultaneously, give rise to sophisticated emergent behaviours that can solve complex 
optimization problems, coordinate group movements, and adapt to changing environmental 
conditions. 

The power of swarm intelligence lies in its scalability and robustness, as these algorithms typically 
become more effective as the number of participating agents increases, and they maintain 
functionality even when individual agents fail or leave the system. This scalability makes swarm 
intelligence particularly well-suited to large-scale robotic applications where traditional 
centralised optimisation approaches become computationally intractable. 

5.4.1 Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO) 
Particle Swarm Optimization represents one of the most successful and widely applied swarm 
intelligence algorithms, originally inspired by the social behaviour observed in bird flocking and 
fish schooling. In PSO, individual agents (particles) explore a solution space by adjusting their 
position and velocity based on their own historical best performance, the best performance 
achieved by their neighbours, and random perturbations that encourage exploration of new 
regions. 

The mathematical elegance of PSO lies in its simple velocity update equation that balances 
exploitation of known good solutions with exploration of new possibilities. Each particle 
maintains its current position, velocity, personal best position, and knowledge of the global or 
local neighbourhood best position. The velocity update combines these components using 
weighted coefficients that control the relative influence of personal experience, social learning, 
and random exploration.

In robotic applications, PSO can be adapted to solve diverse optimization challenges including 
path planning, where robots collectively explore different routes to find optimal paths through 
complex environments; formation control, where swarms optimize their spatial arrangements to 
maximize sensor coverage or communication efficiency; and resource allocation, where robots 
coordinate to distribute tasks or share limited resources optimally. 
The distributed nature of PSO makes it particularly attractive for robotic swarms because each 
robot can execute the algorithm independently using only local information and limited 
communication with neighbours, avoiding the bottlenecks and single points of failure associated 
with centralized optimization approaches. Advanced variants of PSO incorporate adaptive 
parameters, dynamic neighbourhood topologies, and multi-objective optimization capabilities 
that enable robots to balance competing objectives such as energy efficiency, task completion 
time, and system robustness.
