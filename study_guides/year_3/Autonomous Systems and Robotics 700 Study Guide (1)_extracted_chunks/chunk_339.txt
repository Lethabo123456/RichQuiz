5.14.2 System-Level Resilience Assessment 
System-level resilience assessment evaluates the collective ability of cooperative robotic systems 
to maintain functionality and adapt to various disturbances, failures, and environmental changes 
that affect multiple system components simultaneously. This analysis extends beyond individual 
component failures to examine how systems respond to cascading failures, environmental 
disturbances, and operational stress that can affect large portions of the system simultaneously. 
Cascading failure analysis examines how individual robot failures can trigger additional failures 
throughout the system, potentially leading to complete system collapse despite initially localized 

problems. These cascading effects can occur through various mechanisms including 
communication network partitioning where the failure of key communication nodes isolates 
groups of robots, computational overload where the failure of some robots increases the 
computational burden on surviving robots beyond their capacity, and coordination breakdown 
where the failure of coordinator robots leads to system-wide coordination failures. 
Environmental resilience assessment evaluates how cooperative systems respond to 
environmental changes and disturbances that affect system operation but do not necessarily 
cause individual robot failures. 

These environmental factors may include communication 
interference that degrades coordination effectiveness, physical obstacles that disrupt planned 
robot movements, changing weather conditions that affect sensor performance, and dynamic 
operational requirements that require system adaptation and reconfiguration. 
Adaptive capacity analysis examines the system's ability to reconfigure itself in response to 
failures and environmental changes, maintaining effectiveness despite reduced capabilities or 
changed operational conditions. This adaptation may involve dynamic role reassignment where 
robots take on new responsibilities to compensate for failed teammates, formation 
reconfiguration where robot spatial arrangements are adjusted to maintain communication 
connectivity or sensor coverage, and objective modification where system goals are adjusted to 
match current system capabilities. 

The measurement of system resilience requires comprehensive metrics that capture both the 
immediate impact of failures and disturbances and the system's recovery characteristics over 
time. These metrics may include performance degradation curves that show how system 
effectiveness changes following various failure scenarios, recovery time measurements that 
indicate how quickly systems can adapt to new conditions, and stability analysis that examines 
whether systems reach new stable operating points or continue to degrade over time.
