1.4.1 Chronological Milestones 

1.4.1.1 Ancient Automatons (Pre-Industrial Era)  
The Antikythera mechanism was a complex gear-driven device built by the Greeks around 100 BCE to 
predict astronomical events. Although not a robot in the modern sense, this ancient device showcased 
early efforts to automate complex tasks. Similarly, ancient Chinese and Egyptian cultures engineered 
automatons for entertainment or ritual purposes, which performed repetitive tasks through mechanical systems.

1.4.1.2 Industrial Revolution (19th-20th Century)  
The Industrial Revolution in the 18th century marked the true beginning of automation. Machines like the 
spinning jenny and the steam engine were used to increase manufacturing efficiency, thereby reducing 
reliance on human labour. Automation during this period focused on mechanical systems that performed 
tasks previously done by hand, and this shift laid the groundwork for modern robotics by showcasing how 
machines could be used to optimise labour and productivity.
