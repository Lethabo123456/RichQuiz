5.3.2 Raft Consensus Algorithm 
The Raft consensus algorithm represents a more practical approach to distributed consensus that 
prioritises simplicity, understandability, and ease of implementation while providing strong 
consistency guarantees for most real-world scenarios. Raft was specifically designed to address 
the complexity issues that made earlier consensus algorithms like Paxos difficult to understand 
and implement correctly, making it particularly attractive for robotic applications where 
development teams need reliable consensus mechanisms without excessive implementation 
complexity. 

Raft divides the consensus problem into three separable subproblems: leader election, log 
replication, and safety. This decomposition creates a clear architectural framework where one 
agent acts as a designated leader responsible for coordinating decisions and managing the 
consistency of shared state, while follower agents implement the leader's decisions and 
participate in leader election when necessary. This approach naturally maps to many robotic 
coordination scenarios where hierarchical organisation can simplify complex multi-agent 
behaviours. 
The leader election component of Raft ensures that the system can automatically recover from 
leader failures by selecting a new leader through a randomised election process that prevents 
split votes and ensures rapid convergence to a single leader. In robotic swarms, this mechanism 
enables automatic reconfiguration when the coordinating robot experiences hardware failure, 
battery depletion, or communication loss, ensuring that the swarm can continue operating 
effectively without human intervention. 
Log replication in Raft provides the mechanism for distributing decisions and state updates from 
the leader to all followers, ensuring that all agents maintain consistent copies of shared 
information. This replication process includes sophisticated safety mechanisms that prevent data 
loss and ensure that committed decisions cannot be reversed, even during leader transitions or 
network partitions.
