2.2  Sensor Simulation in Autonomous Systems and Robotics 

Sensor Simulation refers to the computational modelling of sensors to replicate their behaviour 
in virtual environments, creating synthetic sensor data that mimics real-world sensor outputs. 
This process generates virtual sensor data such as LIDAR point clouds, camera images, and depth 
maps while accurately accounting for key sensor characteristics including range, resolution, noise, 
and environmental interactions. 

Sensor simulation is essential for designing, training, and validating autonomous systems, 
allowing developers to test perception algorithms without requiring physical hardware. It 
provides safe, scalable, and controlled environments where developers can conduct testing, 
development, and validation of perception algorithms in repeatable and cost-effective virtual 
settings. 

This capability is critical for autonomous systems and robotics, as it enables comprehensive 
algorithm development and validation before deployment on physical hardware. By offering 
controlled virtual environments, sensor simulation supports the entire development lifecycle of 
autonomous systems, from initial algorithm design through final validation, ensuring robust 
performance when systems are eventually deployed in real-world scenarios.
