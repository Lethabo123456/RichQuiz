On the other hand, fully automated systems epitomise the integration of advanced technologies 
like Artificial Intelligence (AI), machine learning, and robotics to perform tasks independently 
without human intervention.  

These systems are characterised by their ability to operate autonomously, requiring minimal to 
no human input once operational. Fully automated systems excel in environments where speed, 
precision, and efficiency are critical. For instance, autonomous vehicles, such as Tesla’s self-driving 
cars, navigate complex environments and make real-time decisions using sensors, algorithms, and 
AI, eliminating the need for a human driver. Similarly, smart factories leveraging Industry 4.0 
principles employ robotics and AI to streamline production lines, achieving unparalleled levels of 
productivity and quality control. 

In finance, algorithmic trading systems autonomously execute 
buy and sell orders based on complex data-driven strategies, responding to market changes faster 
than human traders. The advantages of fully automated systems are undeniable. They offer high 
scalability, enabling operations to expand without a proportional increase in resource use or 
costs. These systems also operate continuously without fatigue, ensuring consistent output 
around the clock. 

Moreover, they significantly reduce human error, as decisions and actions are 
guided by precise algorithms rather than fallible human judgment. However, the transition to fully 
automated systems comes with its challenges. The initial investment required for development, 
deployment, and maintenance is substantial, often limiting accessibility to well-funded 
organisations or industries. Moreover, these systems can raise ethical and safety concerns, 
particularly in critical applications like healthcare or transportation. Failures or unexpected 
behaviours in autonomous systems can have catastrophic consequences, as seen in incidents 
involving self-driving cars. Additionally, the reduced human involvement in fully automated 
systems raises concerns about job displacement and the erosion of skills traditionally performed 
by humans.
