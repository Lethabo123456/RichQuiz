5.5.2 Consensus-Based Task Assignment 
Consensus-based task assignment algorithms enable groups of agents to collectively agree on 
task allocations through iterative negotiation processes that consider both individual agent 
preferences and global system objectives. These approaches are particularly valuable when tasks 
require coordination among multiple agents or when fairness considerations are important in 
addition to efficiency optimisation. 

The mathematical foundation of consensus-based assignment typically involves distributed 
optimization algorithms where agents iteratively update their task preferences and allocation 
proposals based on information received from other agents. These algorithms must carefully 
balance convergence speed with solution quality, ensuring that the system reaches stable 
allocations within reasonable time frames while avoiding suboptimal local minima that could 
result from premature convergence. 

Distributed consensus protocols provide the communication and coordination framework for 
these algorithms, enabling agents to share allocation proposals, communicate constraints and 
preferences, and collectively converge to mutually acceptable solutions. These protocols must 
handle challenges such as message delays, communication failures, and agents joining or leaving 
the system dynamically. 

The Hungarian algorithm and its distributed variants represent one important class of consensus-
based assignment methods that can guarantee optimal solutions for bipartite matching problems 
where tasks must be assigned to agents in one-to-one relationships. Distributed implementations 
of these algorithms enable large-scale task allocation while maintaining optimality guarantees, 
though at the cost of increased communication overhead and computational complexity.
