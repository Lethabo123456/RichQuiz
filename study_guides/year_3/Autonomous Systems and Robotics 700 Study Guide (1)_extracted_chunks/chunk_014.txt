1.3 Main Components of Robots 

Figure 3: Key components of a robot 
 
Just as a house is constructed from various building blocks, robots too are composed of essential 
components that enable them to function and perform their intended tasks. 

This section delves into the core components of robots, exploring their roles and functionalities. 
From sensors that provide the robot's sense of perception to actuators that drive its movements, 
we'll uncover the intricate interplay between these components that brings robots to life. 

1.3.1 Sensors: The Eyes and Ears of Robots 
Sensors serve as the sensory system of robots, enabling them to gather information about their 
surroundings. Just as humans rely on their senses to perceive the world, robots use sensors to 
detect and interpret their environment. Common types of sensors include: 

• Vision Sensors: Cameras and other vision sensors capture visual information, allowing 
robots to see and understand their surroundings. 
• Proximity Sensors: These sensors detect the presence of nearby objects, helping robots 
avoid collisions and navigate safely. 
• Range Sensors: Sensors like ultrasonic and lidar provide distance measurements, enabling 
robots to map their environment and estimate distances accurately. 
• Force Sensors: These sensors measure the force applied to robots, allowing them to 
interact with objects gently and precisely.
