5.15.2 Latency and Synchronisation Costs 
Latency and synchronisation costs analysis examines the time-related overhead associated with 
coordination in cooperative robotic systems, including message transmission delays, processing 
time for coordination decisions, and the costs of maintaining temporal synchronization across 
distributed robot teams. These temporal costs can significantly affect system performance, 
particularly for time-critical applications where coordination delays can compromise mission 
effectiveness. 
Message latency analysis examines the time required for information to travel between robots 
and be processed for coordination decisions, recognizing that coordination effectiveness often 
depends on the timeliness of information sharing. Message latency includes multiple 
components: transmission delay determined by communication channel characteristics and 
distance, queuing delay caused by network congestion and processing backlogs, processing delay 
required for message interpretation and integration into coordination decisions, and propagation 
delay associated with physical signal transmission. 
Synchronisation overhead represents the additional communication and computation required 
to maintain temporal coordination between robots that must execute coordinated actions at 
specific times or in specific sequences. Synchronization protocols require robots to exchange 
timing information, compensate for clock differences, and coordinate their actions despite 
variable communication delays and processing times. The overhead associated with 
synchronization increases with the precision requirements and the number of robots that must 
be synchronized. 
The trade-off between coordination timeliness and communication overhead creates important 
design decisions for cooperative robotic systems, as more frequent communication reduces 
coordination latency but increases bandwidth consumption and processing overhead. This trade-
off is particularly important for dynamic environments where delayed coordination decisions can 
quickly become obsolete, requiring systems to balance the benefits of rapid coordination against 
the costs of increased communication. 
Adaptive communication strategies can optimize the balance between coordination effectiveness 
and communication overhead by dynamically adjusting communication frequency, message 
priority, and synchronization precision based on current operational conditions and coordination 
requirements. These strategies may increase communication frequency during critical 
coordination phases while reducing communication during stable operational periods, optimizing 
system performance across different operational phases.
