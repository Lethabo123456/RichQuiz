5.1.4 Network Topologies for Robot Swarms 
The choice of network topology profoundly affects system behaviour and must consider factors 
such as communication range limitations, environmental obstacles that may block signals, agent 
mobility patterns that change network connectivity dynamically, and scalability requirements as 
the number of agents grows. Advanced topologies implement adaptive mechanisms that can 
reconfigure communication paths based on changing conditions, optimize routing to minimize 
delays and bandwidth usage, and maintain connectivity even as agents move or fail. 

Objectives provide the purposeful direction that drives agent behaviour and system evolution. 
While individual agents may have specific local objectives related to their immediate tasks or 
responsibilities, the system as a whole typically works toward broader collective objectives that 
require coordinated effort and resource sharing among multiple agents. 
The defining characteristics of Multi-Agent Systems emerge from the interplay of these 
fundamental components. Autonomy represents perhaps the most crucial property, as each 
agent must possess the capability to operate independently, making decisions based on its local 
perception of the environment and its understanding of the broader system objectives. This 
autonomy is not absolute isolation but rather intelligent independence that enables agents to 
adapt their behaviour dynamically without requiring constant external supervision or control. 
Reactivity ensures that agents remain responsive to changes in their environment, whether these 
changes result from external factors, the actions of other agents, or the evolution of the task 
requirements. This reactive capability enables the system to adapt to unexpected situations, 
handle dynamic environments, and maintain robust performance even when conditions deviate 
from initial assumptions or plans. 

Proactivity complements reactivity by ensuring that agents do not merely respond to 
environmental stimuli but actively pursue their objectives through goal-directed behaviour. This 
proactive nature enables agents to plan ahead, anticipate potential problems, and take initiative 
in pursuing both individual and collective objectives, creating a system that is not just responsive 
but genuinely intelligent and forward-thinking.
 
Sociability represents the collaborative dimension of multi-agent behaviour, encompassing the 
ability to communicate effectively with other agents, share relevant information, coordinate 
actions, and negotiate solutions to conflicts or resource allocation problems. This social 
intelligence enables the emergence of sophisticated collective behaviours that would be 
impossible for individual agents operating in isolation.
