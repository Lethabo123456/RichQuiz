3 Motion Planning & Pathfinding  
 
3.1  Introduction 
The ability to navigate through complex environments is fundamental to autonomous systems, 
from industrial robots assembling components on factory floors to self-driving cars weaving 
through urban traffic. At the heart of this capability lies motion planning and pathfinding—a rich 
field of study that bridges theoretical computer science, robotics, and artificial intelligence. 
Motion planning addresses the fundamental question: given a starting configuration and a 
desired goal, how can an agent move through its environment while avoiding obstacles and 
satisfying various constraints? This deceptively simple problem encompasses a vast landscape of 
challenges, from the geometric complexities of high-dimensional configuration spaces to the real-
time computational demands of dynamic environments. 
The field has evolved significantly since its early foundations in the 1980s, when researchers first 
formalised the piano mover's problem—determining whether a piano could be moved from one 
room to another through a series of doorways. Today's motion planning algorithms must handle 
far more sophisticated scenarios: humanoid robots navigating cluttered homes, surgical robots 
operating within the confined spaces of the human body, and swarms of drones coordinating 
their movements through three-dimensional airspace. 
This chapter explores both the theoretical underpinnings and practical implementations of 
motion planning systems. We'll examine classical approaches like potential fields and roadmap 

methods, delve into sampling-based algorithms that have revolutionised the field, and investigate 
how modern machine learning techniques are reshaping our approach to navigation problems. 
Along the way, we'll discover how concepts from differential geometry, graph theory, and 
optimisation theory converge to create elegant solutions to some of robotics' most challenging 
problems. 

Whether you're designing the next generation of autonomous vehicles or simply curious about 
how robots think about movement, this exploration of motion planning will provide you with the 
foundational knowledge and contemporary insights needed to understand this dynamic and 
rapidly evolving field.
