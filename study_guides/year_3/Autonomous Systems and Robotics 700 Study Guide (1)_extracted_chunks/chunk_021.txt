1.4.1.3 Modern Robotics (Late 20th - Early 21st Century)  
The post-war period saw the birth of modern robotics, heavily influenced by advances in 
electronics and computing. In 1954, George Devol invented Unimate, the first industrial robot 
designed to handle repetitive tasks in manufacturing environments. It was installed at a General 
Motors plant in 1961, revolutionising assembly lines by performing hazardous tasks with greater 
precision and reliability than human workers.
 
Another milestone during this time was the development of cybernetics, a field focusing on the 
interaction between humans and machines. Researchers began exploring how machines could be 
designed to mimic biological systems, leading to more sophisticated control systems in robots. 

Characteristics of the Industrial Revolution:  
• Introduction of microprocessors 
• Development of advanced sensors and AI 
• Emergence of collaborative robots (cobots) 
• Expansion into multiple domains: healthcare, space exploration, agriculture
