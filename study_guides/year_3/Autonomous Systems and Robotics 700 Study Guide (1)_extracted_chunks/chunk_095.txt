3.2 Paths
Path planning is the process of determining a feasible route for a robot or autonomous agent to
move from a start point to a goal point while avoiding obstacles. It is a critical task in robotics,
autonomous vehicles, and navigation systems, ensuring safe, efficient, and collision-free
movement in complex environments. 

Path planning involves two main challenges: 
• Feasibility: Finding a path that does not intersect with obstacles. 
• Optimality: Finding the best path according to criteria like shortest distance, minimum 
time, energy efficiency, or smoothness. 

We mainly have graph-based search algorithms and sampling-based algorithms. For this module, 
we will primarily focus on graph-based search algorithms such as A* (A-Star, sometimes written 
as A\*) and Dijkstra’s Algorithm. Before applying these algorithms, the search environment needs 
to first be discretized into graph form. Thereafter, it is treated as normal mathematical graph 
composed of nodes (vertices) and edges. The nodes represent positions or states and the edges 
represent possible connections between the nodes and is associated with “cost” (in the form of 
distance, time or energy).
