6.1.3 Reinforcement Learning in Robotics 
Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers a powerful framework for training autonomous robotic agents 
to learn optimal behaviour by interacting with their environments. Unlike supervised learning, 
which depends on labelled datasets, RL enables agents to learn from trial and error, progressively 
improving their decision-making through experience and reward-based feedback. This is 
especially critical in robotics, where agents must operate under uncertainty, adapt to dynamic 
environments, and balance competing objectives such as task success, energy efficiency, and 
safety.

This section covers two essential algorithms in reinforcement learning: 
• Q-learning – a tabular, model-free, off-policy learning algorithm 
• Deep Q-Networks (DQN) – an extension of Q-learning that leverages deep neural 
networks for scalability in high-dimensional state spaces 
Both are pivotal for enabling robots to make intelligent, autonomous decisions. 

6.1.3.1 
Q-Learning: Tabular, Model-Free Reinforcement Learning 
Q-learning is a foundational algorithm in reinforcement learning, first introduced by Watkins 
(1989) and rigorously analyzed by Sutton and Barto in Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction 
(2018). It is a model-free, off-policy algorithm, meaning it does not require a model of the 
environment and learns the optimal policy independently of the actions taken during exploration. 
The Q-learning algorithm learns an action-value function Q(s,a), which estimates the expected 
cumulative reward of taking action a in state s, and thereafter following the optimal policy.

Use in Robotic Control 
In robotic applications, Q-learning can be applied to: 
• Grid-based navigation: Robot learns to reach a target while avoiding obstacles 
• Battery management: Learn when to recharge based on predicted energy costs 
• Policy optimization: Learn delivery or movement policies that optimize task efficiency
