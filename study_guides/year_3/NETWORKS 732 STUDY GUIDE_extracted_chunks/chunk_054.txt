Escalate If Necessary 
 
If all logical and physical theories are exhausted and the problem persists, it may be time to 
escalate the issue to a colleague or a team with greater technical expertise. 
Support Levels in IT Environments: 
• First-level support: Help desk analysts handle common user issues and perform basic 
diagnostics. 
• Second-level support: Analysts with specialized knowledge in areas like server 
management or routing. They handle complex or unresolved issues passed on from 
ﬁrst-level staﬀ. 
• Third-level support: Highly skilled experts or engineers, often outside the help desk 
team. They are called in for critical or specialized problems (Dean, 2013, p. 617). 
The help desk coordinator plays an essential role by organizing teams, managing scheduling, 
and sometimes providing third-level support in small to mid-sized organizations. 

When to Escalate: 
• When the issue exceeds your technical expertise 
• If multiple users or systems are aﬀected 
• When critical services are interrupted
• If time-sensitive systems (like those for sales or healthcare) are at risk 
 
Your organization may have formal escalation procedures, especially for high-priority clients 
(e.g., an e-commerce customer relying on your infrastructure). But often, judgment and 
technical awareness guide the decision to escalate.
