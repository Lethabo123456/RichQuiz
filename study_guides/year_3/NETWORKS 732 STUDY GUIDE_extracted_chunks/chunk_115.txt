2.5.2 DiffServ (Differentiated Services) 

Differentiated Services (DiffServ) is a straightforward method for addressing Quality of Service 
(QoS) concerns by prioritizing traffic at the Layer 3 (network layer) of the OSI model. Unlike other 
QoS methods that focus only on time-sensitive data like voice and video, DiffServ considers all 
types of traffic. This allows it to give high priority to critical streams—such as voice calls—while 
assigning a lower priority to less essential traffic, like employees browsing the internet during 
lunch breaks. This approach enhances the protection of important, time-sensitive data. To 
implement traffic prioritization, DiffServ uses the DiffServ field within IPv4 packets. Specifically, 
the first 6 bits of this 8-bit field are known as the DSCP (Differentiated Services Code Point), which 
indicate the level of service the packet should receive. In IPv6, a similar function is handled 
through the Traffic Class field. These fields guide routers in determining how to forward each 
data stream based on its priority. DiffServ classifies traffic forwarding into two main types: 
EF (Expedited Forwarding): Ensures that a data stream departs from a node at a guaranteed 
minimum rate, reducing delays and helping the data arrive on time and in the correct order. 
AF (Assured Forwarding): Allows routers to assign different levels of resources to various data 
streams. Although it prioritizes traffic, it doesn’t guarantee timely or sequential delivery under 
heavy network load. 
While this explanation simplifies the inner workings of DiffServ, it highlights the key idea: a 
flexible and efficient method of traffic management. Thanks to its simplicity and minimal 
processing requirements, DiffServ is especially well-suited for large-scale, high-traffic networks.
