Correlating Timing and Frequency 
 
Symptoms that occur at speciﬁc times can uncover hidden causes. For instance: 
 
• Slow logins every morning at 8:00 a.m. might point to insuﬃcient server capacity. 
• A weekly network failure at noon may correspond with power ﬂuctuations during 
scheduled tests. 

By tracking time-based patterns, technicians can better predict, isolate, and prevent future 
incidents.

Asking the Right Questions 
 
When users report problems, it’s vital to ask probing and speciﬁc questions. For example, if 
a user can’t retrieve email: 

• Ask when the issue began. 
• Inquire whether others nearby are aﬀected. 
• Review any changes made to the system that day. 
 
In this case, if multiple users are aﬀected and a technician had recently installed unrelated 
software, focus should shift to the shared network segment, not just the user’s individual 
machine.
