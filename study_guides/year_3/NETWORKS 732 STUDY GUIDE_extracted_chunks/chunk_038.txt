Avoiding Premature Conclusions 
 
One of the most common pitfalls is jumping to conclusions. For instance, if several users from 
a speciﬁc department report printing issue, and a technician is already working on a related 
printer problem, it may be tempting to assume all reports are related. However, a new 
complaint may come from a diﬀerent department entirely and indicate a broader network 
failure (Dean, 2013, p. 598). 
Each symptom must be treated as unique but potentially related, and assumptions should be 
avoided unless veriﬁed by clear evidence. 
 
Technicians should be attentive to: 
 
• User explanations of symptoms 
• System behaviours 
• Error messages 
 
Users should be encouraged to read messages aloud or send screenshots. Tools such as the 
Snipping Tool on Windows or Command+Shift+3 on macOS make it easy to capture error 
screens (Dean, 2013, p. 599). Keeping such screenshots as part of the troubleshooting notes 
can help document the problem and improve future responses.
