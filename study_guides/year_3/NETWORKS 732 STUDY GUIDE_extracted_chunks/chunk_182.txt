4.1.2 Multipathing 
Multipathing is the process of establishing multiple network paths between a system and its 
storage device to ensure high availability and redundancy. Its main purpose is to provide an 
alternate route in case the primary connection fails. For example, a SCSI hard drive might be 
connected to two SCSI controllers on the same host, or a disk could be linked to two Fibre Channel 
ports.
 
One of the beneﬁts of virtualization is that it simpliﬁes the setup of multipathing conﬁgurations. As 
shown in the figure below, both Host A and Host B are equipped with multiple host bus adapters 
(HBAs), multiple switches, and connections to more than one storage processor. This results in a 
fault-tolerant system that can withstand failures in HBAs, switches, storage processors, or data 
paths.
 
However, a key disadvantage of multipathing is the added complexity in conﬁguration and 
maintenance. Managing multiple paths and ensuring proper load balancing and failover support 
can increase administrative overhead and may require specialized tools or expertise.

4.1.3 NIC Teaming 
NIC teaming combines multiple network interface cards (NICs) into a single group to improve 
bandwidth and provide failover in case of hardware failure. Teams can be conﬁgured as 
active/active for load balancing or active/passive, where one NIC remains on standby. 
Data is typically sent using multicast, but broadcast can be used to ensure all NICs receive traﬃc 
simultaneously. Teaming can be implemented with one or multiple switches. 
 
However, when using a single switch (static teaming), the setup only protects against NIC failure— 
not switch failure—limiting overall redundancy.
