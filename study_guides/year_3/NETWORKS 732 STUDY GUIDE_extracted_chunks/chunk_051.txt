1.5 Test the Theory to Determine Cause 
 
After establishing a theory regarding the root cause of a network issue, the next step is to test 
that theory. In some cases, this phase also yields the solution, especially if the issue is 
straightforward—like plugging in a disconnected cable. More complex scenarios require 
targeted testing of both physical and logical components (Dean, 2013, p. 612). 
Testing Physical Layer Theories 
 
Physical layer issues are often simple yet prevalent. If a theory points to a hardware fault, 
individual components should be examined or tested to verify functionality. Several practical 
methods are available: 

Ways to Test Physical Connectivity: 

• Use cable testers to evaluate data transmission quality.  
• Inspect LED status lights on interfaces for link and activity indicators. 
• Ensure NICs, SFPs, and GBICs are securely installed. 
• Measure cable distances to conﬁrm they fall within maximum IEEE limits. 
• Utilize a wireless analyser to verify that clients are within range of an access point. 
• Trace cabling from end-to-end between nodes and switches. 

Component swapping is a fast, often eﬀective troubleshooting method. Replacing a suspect 
cable, patch port, or even a NIC with a known-good component can either conﬁrm or rule out 
a hardware failure.
