Delayed Conﬁrmation and Long-Term Observation 
 
Certain ﬁxes require extended observation to verify full success. For example, resolving a 
server overload issue might involve adding processors and enabling symmetric 
multiprocessing. However, unless the same volume of database queries is generated— 
perhaps days or weeks later—it will be diﬃcult to conﬁrm whether the additional resources 
resolved the slowdown. As such, performance issues often demand long-term monitoring to 
verify their resolution. 

Another illustrative case involves performance optimization. Suppose users across the LAN 
experienced sluggish ﬁle access over the past month. You replaced the existing server with 
one that has enhanced CPU power, more memory, and dual NICs. After deployment, you 
would need to conﬁrm that all users can now access ﬁles faster and more reliably—potentially 
seeing an 80% improvement in speed. This outcome would validate both your diagnosis and 
solution.
