3.10.2 Network Hardening 
Firewalls are tools that you can implement to prevent any unauthorized users roaming around on 
public networks from gaining access to your private network. Firewalls can be either stand-alone 
devices or combined with another hardware device, like a server or a router. When developing 
rules for ﬁrewalls, there are two basic approaches, namely explicit deny and implicit deny. Under 
explicit deny, all traﬃc is allowed unless it is specially (explicitly) denied with a rule. This is also 
referred to as blacklisting in that you are creating a blacklist of denied traﬃc. The implicit deny 
denies all traﬃc unless it is speciﬁcally or implicitly allowed by a rule. This is also called 
whitelisting, in that you are creating a whitelist of allowed traﬃc. 

3.10.3 Wireless Security 
Wireless networks are naturally exposed to various threats because their signals travel through 
the air, but using layered techniques can signiﬁcantly reduce these risks. MAC ﬁltering helps limit 
which devices can connect, while antenna placement and power level adjustments control how 
far the wireless signal reaches. Wireless client isolation ensures they can’t communicate directly, 
and guest network isolation keeps visitor traﬃc completely separate from internal systems. 
Preshared keys (PSKs) and stronger protocols like EAP provide authentication and encryption to 
keep unauthorized users out. 

3.10.4 Authentication and Access Control 
Role-based access control (RBAC) is commonly used in networks to simplify the process of 
assigning new users the permissions required to perform a job role. This is not a perfect solution, 
however, and it carries several security issues. First, RBAC is only as successful as the 
organization's policies designed to support it. Poor policies can result in the proliferation of 
unnecessary roles, creating an administrative nightmare for the person managing user access. 
This can lead to mistakes that reduce rather than enhance access security.
