Cable Continuity Testers 

Cable continuity testers are vital for conﬁrming that signals can travel from one end of a 
copper or ﬁbre-optic cable to the other without interruption. These devices apply voltage (or 
light, for ﬁbre cables) and measure whether it’s detectable at the opposite end. They are 
invaluable for identifying breaks, mis-wires, or shorts in cabling. 
Basic testers oﬀer pass/fail indicators with lights or sounds, while more advanced models can 
also verify proper wire pairing in UTP and STP cables according to standards such as TIA/EIA 
568. Even commercially pre-made cables should be tested prior to deployment to ensure 
quality and reduce future troubleshooting.

Cable Performance Testers 

When more detailed diagnostics are required—such as measuring signal strength, 
attenuation, or identifying near-end cross talk—a cable performance tester is used. These 
tools are more advanced and expensive than continuity testers. Many include a TDR (Time 
Domain Reﬂectometer), which sends a signal down a cable and measures the reﬂected signal 
to identify faults and their distances from the source. 
Fiber-optic equivalents, known as OTDRs (Optical TDRs), use light signals to detect breaks, 
bends, and splicing errors. These tools can also validate whether installed cabling meets 
performance standards like Cat 5e or Cat 6a, providing detailed reports and pass/fail ratings 
for compliance.
