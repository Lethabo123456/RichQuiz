2.5.3 CoS (Class of Service) 

Class of Service (CoS) is often mistakenly used interchangeably with Quality of Service (QoS), but 
there is a key difference between the two. QoS encompasses a broad set of techniques applied 
across multiple layers of the OSI model using various protocols to manage and prioritize network 
traffic. CoS, on the other hand, is a specific method used within Layer 2 of the OSI model, focusing 
specifically on Ethernet frames. CoS is commonly applied to optimize the routing of Ethernet 
traffic between VLANs (Virtual LANs). Tagged Ethernet frames—those addressed to specific 
VLANs—contain a 3-bit field in their headers known as the PCP (Priority Code Point). CoS uses 
this field to assign one of eight priority levels (ranging from 0 to 7), which tells the switch how to 
handle the frame when there is congestion at a port. If a switch port receives more data than it 
can immediately forward, it refers to the priority level set by CoS to decide whether to queue the 
frame for later transmission or discard it, depending on its importance. For QoS benefits to be 
realized, all network devices and client systems must support the same protocols. While CoS is 
one approach, modern networks often combine multiple QoS methods to manage traffic 
effectively across different layers and types of infrastructure.
