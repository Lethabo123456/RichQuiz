2.5.1 Traffic Shaping 
When a network experiences high volumes of traffic, users benefit from bandwidth management 
techniques such as traffic shaping. Also known as packet shaping, traffic shaping involves 
controlling specific aspects of data packets, streams, or connections to regulate the type and 
amount of traffic moving across a network or interface at any given time. The main objective is 
to prioritize critical traffic for timely delivery while enhancing overall network performance. 
Traffic shaping may include: 

• Buffering (delaying less-critical traffic) 
• Prioritizing more important traffic 
• Limiting the volume of traffic entering or leaving an interface within a set time frame 
• Restricting the instantaneous data transfer rate (throughput) on an interface 
 
The last two methods fall under a subset of traffic shaping known as traffic policing, which differs 
in that it discards excess traffic instead of delaying it. For instance, an Internet Service Provider 
(ISP) may set a maximum bandwidth limit for a customer to prevent them from using more than 
their allocated share of the Wide Area Network (WAN). This practice helps the ISP forecast the 
capacity it needs to lease from upstream providers and reduces costs by not having to plan for 
all customers using full bandwidth simultaneously—a scenario that's unlikely. Some ISPs allow 
users to select their maximum daily data volume or peak throughput rate, with pricing based on 
the chosen limits. More advanced forms of traffic policing adapt in real-time to current network 
conditions. For example, an ISP might temporarily allow customers to exceed their normal limits
