3.3 Technology-based attacks(threats) 
Technology-based attacks exploit vulnerabilities in software and the communication protocols 
that the systems rely on. This is in contrast to attacks that target environmental or human 
weaknesses 

3.3.1 Denial-of-Service (DoS) 
A DoS is a network security attack in which the hacker makes the system or data unavailable to 
legitimate users by ﬂooding the server with fake traﬃc. Today, DoS attacks are commonly 
launched against a major organization’s intranet and websites. Furthermore, DoS attacks are 
launched in various types, namely protocol-based attacks, volumetric-based attacks, and 
application-layer attacks. 

The Volume-based attack aims to overwhelm the bandwidth of the target with a large amount of 
traﬃc. One example of this type of DoS is the ampliﬁed attack that exploits misconﬁgured servers 
to send ampliﬁed traﬃc to the victim. 
Meanwhile, the protocol-based attack exploits vulnerabilities in layer 3 and 4 protocols. A Smurf 
attack sends ICMP requests to a network’s broadcast address with the victim’s spoofed IP, so all 
hosts reply to the victim. Another attack of the same type is the Ping of Death, which sends 
malformed or oversized ping packets that may crash or freeze the target system. Lastly, the SYN 
ﬂood exploits the TCP handshake by sending many SYN requests without completing the 
handshake.

Additionally, the application layer targets applications or services that often require lower 
bandwidth but cause high impact (GeeksforGeeks, 2025). HTTP ﬂood is an example of an 
application-layer attack. Sending a large number of seemingly legitimate HTTP requests with the 
goal of overwhelming and depleting the server’s resources. Lastly, Distributed Denial of Service 
(DDoS) is a type of DOS attack where multiple systems, which are Trojan-infected, target a 
particular system, causing a DoS attack.
