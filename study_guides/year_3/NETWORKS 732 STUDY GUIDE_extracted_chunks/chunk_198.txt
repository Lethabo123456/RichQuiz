4.1.6 Disaster Recovery Metrics and Backup Strategies 
To eﬀectively plan for high availability and disaster recovery, organizations rely on several key 
metrics that deﬁne system resilience and recovery capabilities. 
 
Mean Time to Repair (MTTR) 
 
MTTR refers to the average time needed to repair a failed device or component. It may be 
measured from the moment a failure is detected or from when the repair begins, until the device 
is operational again. In SLAs (Service Level Agreements), MTTR helps deﬁne acceptable repair 
times to avoid service violations. Clarity on when timing starts and ends is crucial. 
Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) 

MTBF is the average time between one failure and the next. It reﬂects overall device reliability 
and includes both the failure and repair duration: 

MTBF = MTTF (mean time to failure) + MTTR 

Recovery Time Objective (RTO) 
RTO is the maximum allowable downtime after a failure or disaster. It deﬁnes how quickly a 
system or service must be restored to avoid unacceptable business impact. 
 
Recovery Point Objective (RPO) 
 
RPO measures how much data loss is acceptable, based on the most recent backup. It reﬂects the 
point in time to which systems must be restored, determining the frequency of backups. 
Network Device Backup and Restore 
 
Backing up a system means preserving both its data and conﬁguration. For network devices like 
routers and switches, conﬁguration ﬁles are often text-based and should be saved regularly— 
especially in multi-admin environments. On servers, a system state backup captures essential 
settings but not user data. A full backup includes both. 
Having these backups allows quick restoration after failure, minimizing downtime by avoiding the 
need to manually reinstall and reconﬁgure systems.
