Pay Attention to the Obvious 
 
One of the most important yet easily overlooked rules is: check the simple things ﬁrst. Many 
experienced professionals can recall wasting hours on complex diagnostics, only to discover 
that the root cause was something as basic as an unplugged network cable. This emphasizes 
the need to conﬁrm fundamental conditions—like power supply, physical connections, and 
cable integrity—before diving into more advanced analysis (Dean, 2013, p. 604). 
Key Point: Always check the obvious before assuming a complex cause. 
 
1.2 Identify the Problem and Its Symptoms 
 
The ﬁrst and most critical step in troubleshooting any network issue is to identify the problem 
and its symptoms before attempting to deduce the cause. For example, if a user reports they 
are unable to save a ﬁle to a network drive, the symptom might stem from a deeper issue— 
such as the client being unable to access the drive due to hardware failure, misconﬁguration, 
or even user error.
 
A helpful approach is to distinguish between what the user perceives and what is technically 
occurring, listing all plausible causes while ruling out unrelated failures (e.g., printer or 
Internet outages if only ﬁle access is aﬀected).
