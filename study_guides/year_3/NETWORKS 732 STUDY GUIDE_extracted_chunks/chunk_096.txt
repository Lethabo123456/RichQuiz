CHAPTER 2: NETWORK MANAGEMENT 
 
2.1. Fundamentals of Network Management 

Network management is an umbrella term with various interpretations among networking 
experts. In its most expansive sense, it involves evaluating, tracking, and sustaining all facets of a 
network. Tasks may include identifying hardware issues, ensuring critical 
applications operate with high quality of service (QoS), maintaining records of network assets 
and software setups, and choosing optimal times for router upgrades. The techniques for 
network management vary depending on the network's scale and significance. For instance, in 
large networks, administrators might employ management applications that constantly monitor 
devices and connections to ensure they meet performance expectations. If a device doesn't meet  
the response time, the application can automatically alert the responsible network administrator. 

Conversely, in smaller networks, extensive network management may not be cost-effective, such 
networks might utilize a more affordable application that intermittently tests devices and 
connections merely to confirm they remain operational. Numerous disciplines are encompassed 
within the realm of network management, including posture assessments. These disciplines 
collectively aim to enhance operational efficiency and performance while mitigating the risks of 
costly downtime or data loss. Ideally, network management achieves these objectives by 
enabling administrators to anticipate potential issues before they manifest.
