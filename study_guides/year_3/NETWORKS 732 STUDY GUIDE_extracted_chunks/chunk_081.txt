1. Network Monitors 

Network monitors are software-based tools used to analyse traﬃc on a given segment. They 
capture and log frames, detect anomalies, and help visualize traﬃc volume or types. Some 
can recreate network conditions or simulate loads to test capacity and performance. 
Key features include capturing broadcast/multicast packets, logging traﬃc per node, and 
triggering alerts when traﬃc thresholds are met. These tools help uncover issues such as high 
collision rates, slow performance, or even compromised devices ﬂooding the network with 
data. By capturing a traﬃc baseline during normal operations, network monitors can later 
compare and quickly ﬂag abnormal behaviour (Dean, 2013). 
 
2. Protocol Analysers 

Protocol analysers, like Wireshark, delve deeper than network monitors by inspecting frames 
up to Layer 7 (Application Layer) of the OSI model. They interpret protocols such as TCP/IP, 
 ARP, and HTTP, allowing network professionals to understand the type, content, and intent of 
data being transmitted. 

These tools are essential for diagnosing advanced problems, such as corrupted packets, 
protocol mismatches, or application-layer errors. Additionally, they can reveal security 
vulnerabilities, such as unencrypted credentials. Sophisticated analysers can even emulate 
traﬃc to replicate errors and test ﬁxes.
