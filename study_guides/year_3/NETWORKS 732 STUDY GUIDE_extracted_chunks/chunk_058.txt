Evaluating Scalability 
A well-planned ﬁx should not just resolve the issue at hand—it should ideally prepare the 
network for future growth. If a long-term solution isn't feasible immediately, a temporary ﬁx 
might be acceptable, provided it's part of a broader strategy. Dean (2013) advises that while 
installing a stopgap component (e.g., a temporary router) might restore functionality quickly, 
it must be followed up with a permanent replacement to ensure long-term reliability. 
 
Cost Considerations 
 
The cost of a solution must be balanced against its necessity and urgency. Replacing a single 
cable is trivial in terms of budget, but large-scale upgrades—such as replacing hundreds of 
NICs—require signiﬁcant resources. Before committing, ask: 

• Is there a cheaper alternative? 
• Can we prioritize the most aﬀected areas ﬁrst? 
• Will the investment still be valid if hardware is replaced soon? 
 
In some cases, improving network segments or upgrading speciﬁc servers may deliver better 
performance returns than a full-scale replacement.
