4.1.5 Facilities and Infrastructure Support 
When infrastructure equipment is purchased and deployed, the ultimate success of the 
deployment can depend on selecting the proper equipment, determining its proper location in 
the facility, and installing it correctly. Let’s look at some common data center and server room 
equipment and a few best practices for managing these facilities. 
Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) 
 
One risk that all organizations should prepare for is the loss of power. All infrastructure systems 
should be connected to uninterruptible power supplies (UPSs). These devices can immediately 
supply power from a battery backup when a loss of power is detected. You should keep in mind, 
however, that these devices are not designed as a long-term solution. They are designed to 
provide power long enough for you to either shut the system down gracefully or turn on a power 
generator. In scenarios where long-term backup power is called for, a gaspowered generator 
should be installed. 

Power Distribution Units (PDUs) 
 
PDUs distribute electrical power from a central source to multiple outlets, typically within a server 
rack. While basic PDUs resemble power strips, enterprise environments use intelligent PDUs that 
oﬀer advanced features like remote power monitoring, outlet-level on/oﬀ switching, and alarm 
notiﬁcations. Many also integrate with environmental sensors to monitor temperature, 
humidity, and airﬂow within the rack. 
In data centers, PDUs are often paired with Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS) to maintain 
consistent power delivery during outages or voltage ﬂuctuations. Power may come from high- 
current circuits across diﬀerent phases or from multiple UPS systems to ensure redundancy. 
Larger PDUs are commonly used to power multiple server cabinets. These may be stand-alone 
units equipped with main circuit breakers, individual circuit protection, and power monitoring 
panels. Each row or cabinet may have a dedicated PDU setup tailored to its load and redundancy 
needs.
