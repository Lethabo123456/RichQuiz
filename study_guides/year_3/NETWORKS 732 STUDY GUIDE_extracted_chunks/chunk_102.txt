The more comprehensive the data you collect while defining your network’s baseline, the more 
precise your projections will be. Predicting network traffic patterns can be challenging because 
user behavior, the impact of emerging technologies, or variations in resource demand over time 
are unpredictable. For example, the earlier scenario assumes the new users will have network 
usage patterns identical to the existing users. However, new users might cause significantly more 
or less network traffic. How can you collect baseline data on your network? While theoretically, 
it's possible to use a network monitor or analyzer and log its output periodically, many software 
solutions are available to perform baselining tasks for you. These options range from free 
software you can download from the Internet to sophisticated, customizable hardware and 
software combinations. Before selecting a network-baselining tool, it's crucial to assess its 
intended usage. For a small network offering a single crucial application to users, a cost-effective 
tool may suffice. Conversely, if you manage a WAN with multiple critical links, exploring a more 
comprehensive solution is advisable. The baselining tool should also be capable of gathering 
necessary statistics. For instance, only advanced tools can assess the traffic from each network 
node, filter traffic by protocol types and errors, and simultaneously collect statistics from multiple 
network segments.
