3. Wireless Network Testers 

For wireless networks, specialized testers are necessary because wired tools like multimeters 
or continuity testers do not apply. Wireless testers scan radio frequencies to identify access 
points, signal strength, noise, and interference levels. They can verify coverage, help position 
access points, and ensure devices are connecting to the proper SSIDs. 
Many wireless testers come with spectrum analysis capabilities, useful for detecting 
competing signals or electromagnetic interference. Some are software-based, while others 
are dedicated handheld devices. These tools help maintain optimal performance and 
reliability in dynamic wireless environments. 

4. Butt Set 

Used primarily by telephone technicians, a butt set (also known as a lineman’s handset) is a 
durable, telephone-like device designed to connect directly to telephone transmission wires. 
It allows technicians to listen for dial tones, test voice lines, and detect line noise or other 
audio anomalies. The device clips onto wires at various points such as the demarcation point 
(demarc), switching facilities, or central oﬃces, making it especially useful for ﬁeld diagnostics. 
While its primary use is to conﬁrm basic line functionality, some modern models also include 
rudimentary cable testing capabilities. However, butt sets are only eﬀective on demultiplexed 
lines—those that carry a single channel rather than multiplexed signals.
