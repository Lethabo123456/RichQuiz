1.4 Establish a Theory of Probable Cause 

Once the problem’s scope has been identiﬁed, users have been questioned, and recent 
changes assessed, the next step is to formulate a theory of probable cause. This step involves 
using logic, observation, and technical insight to determine the most likely reason behind the 
issue. 
 
Re-Create the Symptoms 
 
One eﬀective method to better understand the cause is to reproduce the symptoms at the 
aﬀected device. If symptoms can’t be reproduced, the issue may have been a one-time 
occurrence or user error. Recreating the issue under controlled conditions helps conﬁrm 
whether the problem is persistent and under what circumstances it occurs. 

Ask These Questions When Reproducing Symptoms: 
 
• Do symptoms recur every time? 
• Do they occur some of the time? 
• Are they triggered under speciﬁc conditions? 
• Do they depend on how many or which programs are running? 
• Can you trigger the same error? 
 
Use the exact user actions that led to the issue. For instance, if a user loses network 
connectivity while browsing, test her workstation under the same conditions and with similar 
websites. 
Use caution when recreating problems that could disrupt services, such as simulated power 
failures or outages.
