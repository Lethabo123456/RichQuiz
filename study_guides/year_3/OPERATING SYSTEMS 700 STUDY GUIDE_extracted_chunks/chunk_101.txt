State Transitions: 
• HOLD to READY: Managed by the Job Scheduler, which ensures sufficient memory and resources 
are available. 
• READY to RUN: Controlled by the Process Scheduler using a predefined scheduling algorithm. 
• RUNNING to READY: Initiated by the Process Scheduler due to a time limit or priority interrupt. 
• RUNNING to WAITING: Triggered by the Process Scheduler when the job requests I/O or another 
resource. 
• WAITING to READY: Managed by the Process Scheduler when the requested resource becomes 
available, signalled by components like the I/O device manager or page fault handler. 
• RUNNING to FINISHED: Initiated by the Process or Job Scheduler when the job completes 
successfully or is terminated due to an error. 
These transitions ensure efficient job management and resource utilization within the operating 
system.

3.3.2 Threads States 
 
Figure 3.2 
 
Threads transition through five primary states during execution: 
 
1. READY: A thread enters this state when resources are allocated after creation. It waits in the 
READY queue until the Process Scheduler assigns it to the processor. 
2. RUNNING: The thread executes tasks. It transitions to: 
 
• WAITING: When waiting for an external event (e.g., mouse click). 
• DELAYED: When processing is delayed for a specified time.  
• BLOCKED: When waiting for an I/O operation to complete. 
 
3. WAITING: Transitions to READY when the awaited event occurs. 
4. DELAYED: Transitions to READY after the delay time elapses. 
5. BLOCKED: Transitions to READY after the I/O operation completes. 
6. FINISHED: When the thread completes execution, its resources are released, and it exits 
the system. 

Efficient state transitions, such as delaying threads instead of looping, optimize system 
performance.
