2.2 Differentiate Fault Tolerance Devices and Interfaces 
Even when dedicated links and VPN connections remain sound, a faulty device or interface in 
the data path can affect service for a user, a whole segment, or the whole network. To 
understand how to increase the fault tolerance of a connection 
from end to end, let’s return to the example of Legend PayNTime. Suppose that the company’s 
network administrator decides to establish a VPN agreement with a national ISP. Legend 
PayNTime bandwidth analysis indicates that a single T1 link is sufficient to transport the data 
of five customers from the ISP’s office to Legend PayNTime data room. Figure 2.3 provides a

● Firewall 
● Router 
● CSU/DSU 
● Multiplexer 
● Switch

To achieve the utmost fault tolerance, each critical device requires redundant NICs, Small 
Form-factor Pluggable SFPs, power supplies, cooling fans, and processors, all of which should 
be able to immediately assume the duties of an identical component, a capability known as 
automatic failover. If one NIC in a router fails, for example, failover ensures that the router’s 
other NIC can automatically handle the first NIC’s responsibilities.
