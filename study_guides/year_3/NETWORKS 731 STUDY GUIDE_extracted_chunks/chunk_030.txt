1.4 Making a  Right Choice when Selecting a Cable Medium 
 
There are two main media types used in structured cabling networks, each with unique 
benefits. Copper (UTP) cables are the favoured option for many organizations due to its 
substantial cost-effectiveness and ease of installation. UTP is a cost-effective choice due to its 
easily accessible components and maintenance tools, as well as its cheaper material costs. The 
majority of technicians have the fundamental tools and abilities needed for an easy 
installation. The cables are straightforward to terminate, flexible, and troubleshoot when 
problems occur. Furthermore, UTP connections maintain backward compatibility with 
previous systems and are universally compatible with current network infrastructure, 
guaranteeing smooth integration in the majority of networking setups. 
 
Although initially more costly, fiber optic cables offer better performance advantages that 
frequently make up for their increased price. They are perfect for high-demand settings 
because of their remarkable bandwidth capacity and quicker data transfer speeds. Fiber's 
capacity to send data over long distances with little signal deterioration, requiring fewer 
repeaters and allowing for larger campus coverage, is one of its most important benefits. In 
areas containing electrical equipment, fiber's tolerance to radio frequency interference (RFI) 
and electromagnetic interference (EMI) makes it incredibly dependable.
