3.5.3 Manipulating   

Manipulating interaction involves people interacting with digital objects by performing actions that 
reflect how they interact with physical objects in the real world. Common actions include moving, 
selecting, opening, and closing objects, as well as more advanced actions like zooming in and out or 
stretching and shrinking—actions not possible with physical objects. 
Some examples of how this form of interaction is used include: 

• VR controllers that allow users to manipulate digital objects via physical remotes or gestures. 
• Gesture control technology in cars, enabling users to interact without physical touch. 
• Tagged physical objects, such as balls or bricks, that trigger digital events when manipulated (e.g., 
causing animations or sounds when moved). 
A key framework that has influenced GUI design is direct manipulation, proposed by Shneiderman in 
1983. It suggests that digital objects should be represented in a way that users can manipulate them 
similarly to physical objects. The core principles of direct manipulation are: 
1. Continuous representation of objects and actions of interest. 
2. Rapid, reversible, incremental actions with immediate feedback. 
3. Physical actions (or button presses) instead of complex commands. 

These principles enable users to directly control digital objects in an intuitive way. For instance, dragging 
a file icon into a folder makes the action immediately visible, reinforcing the user's sense of control and 
progress. 

The benefits of direct manipulation include: 
• Quick learning for beginners. 
• Efficiency for experienced users. 
• Easy recall of functions for infrequent users. 
• Immediate visual feedback, which reduces anxiety and prevents errors. 
• Increased confidence and mastery for users. 

Apps like word processors, video games, learning tools, and graphical editing software all benefit from 
these principles. Scrolling with a finger on a touchscreen or using a mouse on a laptop is also a form of 
direct manipulation, offering a hands-on, intuitive user experience.
