Today, many consultancies specialize in interaction design, including established firms like Nielsen 
Norman Group and IDEO, as well as newer companies focusing on niche areas such as job board software, 
digital media, or mobile design. Smaller consultancies, like Bunnyfoot and Dovetailed, emphasize 
diversity, interdisciplinarity, and scientific research by incorporating psychologists, usability experts, and 
customer experience specialists into their teams. Many of these consultancies maintain impressive 
websites showcasing case studies, tools, and blogs to share insights and contribute to the broader 
conversation on the role of technology in shaping human experiences. For example, Holition publishes 
engaging case studies and videos to explore the cultural and commercial implications of their work. 
 
1.6 People-Centered Design  
People-centered design focuses on understanding users' feelings, satisfaction, and enjoyment when 
interacting with a product. This includes their impressions of how it looks, feels, and operates, down to 
the sensory effects of small details like the smooth rotation of a switch or the satisfying click of a button. 
The quality of the experience—whether quick, like taking a photo; leisurely, like playing with an 
interactive toy; or immersive, like visiting a museum—is central to this approach. As Don Norman (2004) 
emphasized, designing products that are functional, understandable, and usable is not enough; they must 
also bring joy, excitement, pleasure, fun, and beauty to people's lives.
