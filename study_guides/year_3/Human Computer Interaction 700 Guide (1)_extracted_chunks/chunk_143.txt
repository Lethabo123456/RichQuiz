3.5.5 Responding   
 
Responding interaction involves the system proactively initiating interaction by providing information or 
alerts that it deems relevant to the user's current context. This is often based on detecting a user’s 
location, activity, or previous behaviours, without the user needing to ask or command the system. 

Examples of this type of interaction include: 
• Location-based notifications: A system detects that the user is near a coffee bar where friends are 
meeting and notifies them on their phone or watch. 
• Fitness trackers: These devices notify the wearer when they reach milestones, such as walking 
10,000 steps, without the user having to request it. 
• Contextual information: After a user takes a photo of a dog, Google Lens automatically provides 
information about the breed based on the image, showcasing proactive learning from past 
interactions. 
This type of interaction allows systems to provide timely, relevant information or actions that enhance 
user experience by anticipating their needs.
