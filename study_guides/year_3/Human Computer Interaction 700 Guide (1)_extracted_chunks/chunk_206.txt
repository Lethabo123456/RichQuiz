Conversational Design in Digital Interfaces 

Conversational user interfaces (CUIs), such as chatbots, emulate human dialogue by incorporating turn-
taking and repair mechanisms. Advanced systems like Replika analyze conversational patterns through 
machine learning, enabling dynamic interactions that mirror face-to-face communication. 
By understanding and applying these conversational principles, developers can create more engaging 
and effective digital communication tools, from emails to AI-driven chatbots. 
  
Many chatbots are designed to handle customer inquiries, such as providing information on how to 
request a refund for an online purchase. They are typically programmed to address a specific set of 
questions effectively. However, when faced with an unexpected query, they often default to generic 
responses like “I am not sure about that.” 
Beyond simple Q&A functions, ongoing research is exploring more versatile applications for 
conversational agents. For instance, some are being developed to facilitate voice-based work meetings 
by enhancing collaboration among team members working on tasks. A critical aspect of their design 
involves determining the appropriate level of proactivity and the type of facilitation that is most helpful. 
For example, if a group becomes stalled during a decision-making process, the agent might suggest an 
alternative approach. Research (Reicherts et al., 2022) has shown that providing occasional, context-
specific prompts and questions can effectively guide discussions in group settings. However, excessive or 
overly frequent interventions can be counterproductive and irritating. Unlike companion chatbots, which 
aim to replicate human-like conversation, these virtual agents are designed specifically to support and 
steer discussions among meeting participants rather than engage as conversational partners.
