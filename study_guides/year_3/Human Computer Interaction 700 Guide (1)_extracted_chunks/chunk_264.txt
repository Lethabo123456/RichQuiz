6.4.2 Tracking and Reflecting on Moods  
 
There has been considerable interest in using technology to help individuals gain a better understanding 
of their moods and the causes behind mood swings. For example, one might feel happy one day and 
experience a bad mood the next. While facial tracking software can detect specific emotions like 
happiness, it struggles to identify broader mood shifts, as these often don't manifest through noticeable 
physiological responses. A person in a bad mood may choose not to express or even conceal their feelings. 
An alternative approach involves people manually tracking their emotions by journaling or rating their 
mood at specific moments, reflecting on past feelings. One of the first apps designed to support digital 
journaling was Echo, which allowed users to type a subject line, rate their happiness, and add descriptions, 
photos, or videos. Occasionally, it prompted users to reflect on previous entries. The idea was that this 
type of reflection, facilitated by technology, could improve well-being and happiness. Each entry was 
displayed as a stacked card with a timestamp and a smiley happiness rating. Users of Echo reported 
positive effects, such as reliving happy memories and processing negative emotions through writing. This 
dual process of recording and reflecting helped them draw positive lessons from their experiences. 
Since the launch of Echo, several mood-tracking apps, like Moodnotes and Daylio, have been developed 
to assist users in monitoring their moods and understanding why they might feel particularly low or overly 
enthusiastic. The assumption is that by better understanding their emotional states, individuals can 
improve their mental well-being. 

Virtual reality (VR) has also been used to help individuals explore their moods. One example is Mood 
Worlds, developed by Nadine Wagener and colleagues (2022), a VR application that allows users to 
visualize their emotions by creating a personalized virtual space using 3D digital tools. This form of 3D 
digital painting for exploring feelings has been shown to enhance happiness and promote a more positive 
mindset in participants.
