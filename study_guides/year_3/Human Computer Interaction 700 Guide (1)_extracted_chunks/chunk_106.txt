Using prompts to provoke different ways of thinking is another common technique. Prompts can take 
many forms, such as ideation techniques, method cards, or physical materials, and are often used in co-
design workshops or brainstorming sessions. One popular ideation technique is SCAMPER, which helps 
improve existing products or ideas. SCAMPER encourages designers to explore the following actions: Swap 
one element with another; Combine, Adapt, or Modify aspects of the product; Put the product to different 
uses; Eliminate elements; and Rearrange or reverse elements. In interaction design, this might involve 
reconsidering the product's context of use, generating a minimum viable product (Gothelf and Seiden, 
2021), or viewing the product from the perspective of a five-year-old. Additionally, creativity triggers—
simple prompts (Burnay et al., 2016)—can spark novel requirements. These prompts are derived from 
practitioner experience and are typically presented in a standardized form: title, short description, 
guidelines, example, and visual. Figure 2.8 illustrates two examples of these creativity triggers. 
 
A practical answer to where alternative designs come from is that they stem from seeking out different 
perspectives and exploring other existing designs. The process of fostering creativity and inspiration can 
be enhanced by tapping into a designer’s own experiences and studying the ideas and suggestions of 
others. Actively seeking out sources of inspiration is a valuable step in the design process. These sources 
can be products closely related to the new design, such as competitors’ products or earlier versions of 
similar systems, or they could come from entirely different domains. 
However, there are situations where the ability to consider alternative designs is limited. Design often 
involves balancing constraints and making trade-offs between various requirements, which can result in 
fewer viable alternatives. For example, when designing an app for Android smartphones, designers are 
encouraged to align with Android's established look and feel to maintain consistency with the platform's 
branding. Similarly, when updating an existing system, retaining familiar elements to preserve the user 
experience might be prioritized, although the decision to adopt a completely new conceptual model could 
potentially lead to a better product.
