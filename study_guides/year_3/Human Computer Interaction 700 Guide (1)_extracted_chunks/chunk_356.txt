9.2 AgileUX 
 
AgileUX: Integrating UX Design with Agile Development 
AgileUX combines interaction design techniques with agile software development methods. Both 
approaches emphasize iteration, measurable outcomes, and stakeholder collaboration. However, 
integrating UX into agile workflows poses challenges, particularly due to agile's short iteration cycles 
(often 1–4 weeks) and frequent reprioritization, which can hinder comprehensive UX activities. Despite 
these challenges, agile practices such as retrospectives, cross-functional collaboration, and frequent 
feedback loops benefit UX integration. 
Challenges and Insights 
Early concerns (Sharp et al., 2006) centered on agile’s potential to compromise UX quality due to rushed 
activities. However, the flexibility of agile processes, when balanced correctly, allows teams to refine 
practices and improve outcomes. Key findings include: 

1. Understanding Dimensions: Tiago Da Silva et al. (2018) highlight three dimensions for successful 
integration:  
o Process and Practice: Well-understood but not easily implemented. 
o People and Social: Emerging understanding of collaboration. 
o Technology and Artifacts: Needs further exploration for team coordination. 

2. Startups and Lightweight Practices: Startups benefit from adopting lightweight UX methods, such 
as usability testing, to balance rapid iterations with design quality. 
Shifting Perspectives on UX 
Agile development views design artifacts as dynamic tools rather than static deliverables. This shift 
requires UX designers to adapt their workflows, ensuring that research, prototyping, and user testing align 
with agile's pace and evolving priorities. As Greg Nudelman (2019) observes, modern UX prioritizes 
partnerships over deliverables, fostering collaboration between UX designers and engineers. 
Case Example: Group Travel App 
Consider a group travel app with four primary user stories (epics): 
1. Enable group travelers to choose vacations that suit their preferences. 
2. Provide visa restriction information for group members. 
3. Highlight vaccination requirements for chosen destinations. 
4. Allow travel agents to display accurate, updated information. 

Initially, epics related to vacation selection and agent support are prioritized to deliver a minimum viable 
product (MVP). Secondary features, such as vaccination and visa guidance, may be delayed or re-
evaluated based on business value.
