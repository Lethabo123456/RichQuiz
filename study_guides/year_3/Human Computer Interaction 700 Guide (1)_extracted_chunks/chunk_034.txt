Chapter 1: WHAT IS INTERACTION DESIGN? 
 
1.1 Introduction 
 
Every day, we interact with a variety of products, such as smartphones, tablets, smartwatches, and coffee 
machines, to name just a few. Additionally, we use numerous apps and social media platforms, making 
the list of interactive products seemingly endless. Take a moment to consider how easy and enjoyable 
these products are to use. Some, like a tablet, offer a seamless and pleasant experience with simple 
actions like tapping an app or flipping through photos. Others, such as a ticket machine that forces you to 
restart after failing to read your credit card, can lead to significant frustration. 
The difference in usability often lies in the design process. Many products, like smartphones and fitness 
trackers, have been thoughtfully designed with users' needs in mind, making them intuitive and enjoyable 
to interact with. However, other products, such as stoves with confusing time-setting functions, are 
engineered more for functionality than ease of use. These products may work well in terms of 
performance but can be difficult to learn and remember how to use in real-world situations. This often 
leads to unnecessary frustration for users, highlighting the importance of designing for both functionality 
and usability.
