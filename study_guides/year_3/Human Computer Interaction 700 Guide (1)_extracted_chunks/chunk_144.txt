3.6 Paradigms, Visions, Challenges, Theories, Models, and Frameworks  

Other sources of inspiration and knowledge that influence design and guide research include paradigms, 
visions, challenges, theories, models, and frameworks. These vary in their scale and relevance to specific 
problem areas and are used to frame, guide, and define the scope of a research or design project. A 
paradigm refers to a general approach embraced by a community of researchers and designers, based on 
shared assumptions, concepts, values, and practices. A challenge involves tackling critical global issues, 
such as enhancing sustainability and alleviating poverty. A vision presents a future scenario that shapes 
research and development in interaction design, often expressed through a narrative or film. A theory 
provides a well-supported explanation of a phenomenon, such as self-determination theory, which 
explains human motivation and has been applied to analyze human-computer interaction research in 
gaming and play (Tyack and Mekler, 2020). A model is a simplified representation of an aspect of human-
computer interaction, designed to help designers predict and assess alternative designs. A framework 
consists of interrelated concepts or specific questions aimed at informing a particular field, such as 
collaborative learning, or guiding an analytical method, like ethnographic studies. 
  
3.6.1 Paradigms 
Paradigms in interaction design refer to widely accepted approaches and practices that guide research 
and design within a specific community. These include the types of questions to ask, phenomena to 
observe, and ways to analyze findings. Paradigm shifts, such as the move from desktop computing to 
mobile technologies and the rise of AI, mark significant changes in the design and development of 
interactive technologies.
