1.3 Switching to Digital  
(Rogers, Sharp and Preece, 2023) 
Many tasks that once required physical objects are now done digitally. For example, instead of going to a 
machine to buy a ticket or using an ATM to withdraw cash, we now handle these transactions using apps 
on our smartphones or tablets. This shift has made these activities faster, easier, and more convenient. A 
good example is paying for parking via a mobile app. In the past, parking meters required drivers to insert 
coins to park, which could be problematic if they didn't have the right change. 
 
Now, with mobile apps, we can pre-enter our details online and pay for parking directly using a credit card 
or digital payment method. The app stores our information for future use, making the process even 
quicker the next time we need to park. We only need to enter the parking location number, and the app 
fills in the rest of the details. Some apps even send a notification when our parking time is about to expire, 
offering an option to extend it with a simple tap. This digital reminder not only helps prevent parking fines 
but also benefits the parking company by generating more revenue.
