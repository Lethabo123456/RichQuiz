8.2.4 Ethical Considerations of Data Collection and Storage  
In addition to obtaining informed consent, ethical considerations related to data collection and storage 
are critical. Modern technology, suchas smartphones, video conferencing systems, and online platforms, 
makes it easy to capture and store high-quality data, including photographs, videos, audio recordings, and 
even automated transcripts. However, these conveniences come with ethical responsibilities.  
All collected data must be stored securely, and informed consent must be obtained from individuals 
whose data is captured. While consentis straightforward in small, controlled settings, challenges arise 
when data collection occurs in publicspaces with many individuals present. In such cases, it’s essential to 
anonymize data before analysis and carefully review recordings to exclude unnecessary or sensitive 
details that could cause harm or embarrassment if made public. 

Secure data storage is another crucial consideration. Data should be protected against unauthorized 
access, but with many storage systems physically located around the world, compliance with data 
protection laws depends on the jurisdiction. For example, the European Union’s General Data Protection 
Regulation (GDPR), which came into effect in May 2018, provides individuals with significant control over 
their personal data. Researchers must consider whether their storage solutions, such as laptops used for 
data collection, are encrypted and secure. A lost or stolen device, for instance, could pose a significant 
risk if it contains unprotected personal data. 
Organizations and projects collecting personal data that can identify individuals must implement robust 
safeguards. This includes anonymizing data, storing participant names separately from their information, 
securing physical records in locked storage, and encrypting digital files. To address these concerns, 
researchers often develop data management plans that outline data protection measures, as well as 
policies for sharing data with other researchers or projects. These plans help ensure compliance with 
ethical standards and mitigate risks associated with data collection and storage.
