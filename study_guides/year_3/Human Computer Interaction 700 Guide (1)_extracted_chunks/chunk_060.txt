1.9.1 Usability Goals 
 
Usability ensures that interactive products are easy to learn, effective to use, and enjoyable from the 
user’s perspective. It aims to optimize interactions so people can carry out activities effectively in various 
contexts, such as at work, school, or in daily life. Usability is typically broken down into the following goals:

• Effective to use (effectiveness): Can users achieve their goals using the product? 
• Efficient to use (efficiency): Can users accomplish tasks with minimal effort and time? 
• Safe to use (safety): Does the product prevent harm or errors, ensuring the user's well-being? 
• Good utility (utility): Does the product meet the user’s needs and provide necessary features? 
• Easy to learn (learnability): Can users quickly understand how to use the product? 
• Easy to remember how to use (memorability): Can users easily recall how to use the product after 
a period of not using it? 
• Enjoyable to use (satisfaction): Does the product provide a pleasant and engaging experience? 
Usability goals are usually framed as questions to guide designers in assessing the product and user 
experience. This approach helps uncover potential design issues early in the process. For example, instead 
of simply asking if the system is easy to learn, a more detailed question like, “How long will it take for 
someone to figure out how to use the basic functions of a new smartwatch, and how much can they 
leverage their prior experience?” provides deeper insights that can inform better design decisions.
