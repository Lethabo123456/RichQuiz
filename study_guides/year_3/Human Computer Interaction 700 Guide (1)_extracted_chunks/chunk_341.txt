8.5.3 Administering Questionnaires 
 
Reaching a representative sample of participants and ensuring a high response rate are essential for the 
success of a study. In large surveys, respondents must be selected using a sampling technique. Interaction 
designers often use smaller sample sizes, usually fewer than 20 participants, which tend to yield 100% 
completion rates. However, for larger or more remote populations, obtaining a reasonable response rate 
can be challenging, with a 40% return rate generally being acceptable. Incentives may be required to 
encourage participation, depending on the audience. 
While online questionnaires are most common, paper questionnaires are still used in certain contexts, 
such as when participants are in public places or co-located with researchers. Occasionally, short 
questionnaires may be sent within an email for populations unable to access online survey systems. Other 
media, like VR environments, can also be used to deliver questionnaires, blending digital and physical 

interactions. Online questionnaires are interactive and can incorporate features like checkboxes, radio 
buttons, pull-down menus, and videos, along with immediate data validation and automated response 
collection. These features contribute to faster response rates and seamless data analysis. 
 
When deploying an online questionnaire, obtaining a random sample of respondents is often challenging. 
Most online questionnaires rely on convenience sampling, which limits the ability to generalize findings.

To deploy an online questionnaire, follow these steps: 

1. Plan the Survey Timeline: If there is a deadline, work backward from it and create a weekly plan. 
2. Design the Questionnaire Offline: Start with plain text for ease of transfer into the online survey 
tool. 
3. Program the Survey: The time required depends on the survey's complexity, such as the number 
of navigational paths or interactive features. 
4. Test the Survey: Ensure it functions as expected and review the questions with content experts, 
survey experts, and potential respondents. This step often includes a pilot study. 
5. Recruit Respondents: To motivate participants, make the invitations intriguing, simple, friendly, 
and clear. 

Online questionnaire templates offer a variety of question types (e.g., open-ended, multiple-choice), 
rating scales (e.g., Likert, semantic differential), and answer options (e.g., radio buttons, checkboxes, 
drop-down menus). These templates allow widespread administration of the survey and segmentation. 
For example, an airline satisfaction survey might have separate sections for different aspects of the travel 
experience (e.g., check-in, baggage handling, inflight service). Respondents can skip irrelevant sections, 
and the data already provided can still be used for analysis if they drop out.
