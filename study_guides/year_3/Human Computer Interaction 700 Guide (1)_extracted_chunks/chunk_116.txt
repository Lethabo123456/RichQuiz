Chapter 3: CONCEPTUALIZING INTERACTION  
 
3.1 Introduction 
  
When developing new ideas for a design project, it's crucial to conceptualize them in terms of the 
product's functionality, often referred to as creating a proof of concept. This aligns with the Double 
Diamond framework, particularly the "discover" and "define" phases, where designers explore the 
problem and test initial solutions. A proof of concept helps to define the area and provide a reality check, 
assessing the feasibility of the proposed ideas and their desirability and usefulness. It also allows designers 
to articulate the basic components of the product, such as software or user models, and how users will 
understand and interact with it. 
 
For example, if a designer suggests creating a voice-assisted mobile robot to help waiters take orders and 
deliver meals, it's important to ask why this idea is needed. Is there an actual problem to solve, or is the 
idea based on perceived benefits? A problem-focused approach—like the difficulty of recruiting qualified 
waiters—provides clarity and helps guide the design process toward meaningful, practical solutions. The 
goal is to ensure the concept is not only innovative but also addresses real user needs effectively.
