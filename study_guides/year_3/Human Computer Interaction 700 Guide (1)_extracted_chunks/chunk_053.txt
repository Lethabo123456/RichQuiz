1.7 Understanding People 
 
A deep understanding of people and the contexts in which they live, work, and learn is critical for 
designing interactive products that meet their specific needs. For example, the requirements for a 
collaborative space mission planning tool differ greatly from those of a tool for customer and sales agents 
in a furniture store. Recognizing individual differences is key, as one-size-fits-all solutions rarely work.  
Children, for instance, enjoy interactive quizzes and animations, while adults may prefer podcasts or 
straightforward interfaces. Similarly, older adults may not always require larger text or controls, contrary 
to assumptions. Many older users are adept at using standard or small interfaces, like smartphones, but 
may resist adopting new technologies due to concerns about time management and digital distractions. 
Cultural differences also significantly impact interaction design, especially for global products. Variations 
in date and time formats or platform behaviors, such as those seen on Weibo and Twitter, highlight the 
need to tailor designs for diverse user groups. For instance, during the COVID-19 pandemic, Weibo users 
focused more on health-related discussions, while Twitter users engaged with policy and societal issues. 
To address these nuances, designers must consider psychological and social factors, including emotions, 
abilities, desires, and frustrations. Insights into these aspects help designers evaluate alternatives and 

develop effective solutions. By embracing user diversity and cultural sensitivities, designers can create 
products that are universally accepted and meaningful across different contexts.
