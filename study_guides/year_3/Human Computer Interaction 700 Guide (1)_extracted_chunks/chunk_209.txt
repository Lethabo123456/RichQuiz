5.4 Remote Collaboration and Communication 
 
Various social technologies have been created to facilitate remote communication and collaboration, 
either by replicating the dynamics of face-to-face interactions or by enhancing the ways we currently 
connect and engage. The following sections explore how these technologies accomplish these goals. 

5.4.1 Videoconferencing 
 
Videoconferencing is an online technology that allows individuals in different locations to connect and 
interact over the Internet. Significant research in the 1980s and 1990s focused on developing innovative 
systems to simulate the experience of being in the same physical space while communicating remotely. 
One notable example was the VideoWindow system (Fish et al., 1990), designed to link two lounge areas 
50 miles apart. It utilized a large 3-foot by 5-foot picture window to project video images of each location, 
enabling users to see people in the other room at a scale similar to real life. Studies of its use revealed 
that many conversations between remote participants were almost indistinguishable from face-to-face 
interactions, with the primary differences being slightly louder speech and frequent discussions about the 
video system itself (Kraut et al., 1990). 
Further research on videoconferencing interactions found that users tended to project themselves more, 
take longer conversational turns, and interrupt each other less frequently compared to in-person 
conversations (O’Connaill et al., 1993). These findings highlighted the nuanced ways in which technology 
shapes remote communication dynamics.
