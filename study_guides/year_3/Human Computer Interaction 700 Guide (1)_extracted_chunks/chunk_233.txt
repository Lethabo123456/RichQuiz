5.6 Social Games  
Social games, whether traditional or online, are designed to foster social interaction, and they can be 
played with or without technology. These games may involve two or more players who either cooperate 
or compete, and they are typically designed to make players aware of each other's actions and 
performance. In many cases, these games allow players to interact with each other during the gameplay, 
such as in popular video games like Fortnite, Roblox, and Minecraft, where players can chat while playing. 
Discord is the most popular platform for real-time communication in these games, allowing players to talk 
through voice or text. Some games even provide features like a "whisper" function, which allows players 
to communicate directly within a larger group chat. 
There are also face-to-face social games augmented by technology. A classic example is Rock Paper 
Scissors played as an Alexa skill, where Alexa vocalizes each round, and co-located players make their own 
gestures. A more advanced version is the Rock Paper Scissors Lizard Spock skill, where Alexa tracks the 
results over multiple rounds, creating an enjoyable and interactive experience. 
Social online games are also designed to facilitate community-building, with a mix of competition, 
collaboration, and social dynamics like peer pressure and rebellion. Matt Richetti, a social game designer, 
offers three heuristics to assess how social a game is: 

• Synchronous or Asynchronous Interaction: Whether players interact in real-time or take turns. 
• Symmetrical or Asymmetrical Social Interaction: Whether forming relationships requires input 
from both parties or can be initiated unilaterally. 
• Strong or Weak Ties: Whether relationships formed are deep and lasting or temporary. 
An example of a unique social game is Journey, created by Jenova Chen. In this game, players control 
robed figures traveling toward a distant mountain. They can encounter and help each other, but 
communication is limited to a musical chime, fostering connection through shared experience rather than 
verbal communication. 

In addition to gaming, live streaming has emerged as a form of social interaction. Gamers broadcast their 
activities to a live audience on platforms like Twitch or YouTube, where viewers can interact with the 
streamer and each other through chat. This two-way communication creates a sense of community, 
especially when there is a small, dedicated audience who interact regularly, much like a group watching 
a local community event.
