The benefits of working from home have proven to be numerous, including flexible schedules, the ability 
to wear casual clothes, fewer distractions from colleagues, and the absence of commuting. Additionally, 
employees save money by not having to travel daily. Consequently, after the pandemic, many people 
sought to continue working from home, with the option to come into the office one or two days a week. 
This led to the rise of hybrid working, designed to combine the best of both worlds—allowing employees 
to work from home part of the week while encouraging in-office presence on certain days to foster a 
sense of community. While some found hybrid work to improve their work-life balance, others found it 
emotionally draining and missed the casual conversations and informal meetings that occur in an office 
setting. 

However, hybrid work is still evolving. For many remote workers, it often feels like watching a meeting 
through a narrow straw, viewing pixelated and poorly framed webcam images of colleagues (Microsoft, 
2022). Additionally, some employees, due to personal reasons or lack of space at home, preferred to 
return to the office each day, only to find themselves in back-to-back videoconferencing meetings with 
colleagues working remotely. 

To address this imbalance, Microsoft has been researching ways to make hybrid meetings more equitable 
and inclusive. Their efforts have focused not just on redesigning physical meeting spaces but also on 
improving the digital communication and collaboration experience. One of their innovations involved 
changing the placement of video feeds for remote participants on shared screens. Instead of positioning 
them at the top, they placed the feeds at the bottom to improve eye contact, making it more natural as 
they aligned with in-room participants. Documents are also displayed in the remaining space, allowing 
real-time annotations and updates based on the team’s current work.
