Automated facial coding has become increasingly popular in commercial applications, particularly in 
marketing and e-commerce. For example, Affectiva's media analytics software (www.affectiva.com) 
utilizes computer vision and deep learning algorithms to track emotional reactions to digital content via 
a webcam. This technology analyzes user engagement with content such as movies, shopping sites, and 
ads, classifying emotions like anger, contempt, disgust, fear, joy, and sadness. The emotions detected are 
represented as percentages on the display above the person’s face. 

For example, Figure 6.9 might show a 100% happiness label and 0% for the other categories, with white 
dots on the face marking the key facial features being analysed, such as smiling, eye widening, brow 
raising, and mouth opening. These markers help determine the facial expression, allowing the system to 
infer emotions. If a person furrows their brow and wrinkles their nose (suggesting disgust) when an ad 
appears, or smiles (indicating happiness), the system can adjust the content accordingly, such as changing 
the advertisement or movie storyline to better align with the person’s emotional state. 
Affectiva also applies this technology to monitor drivers’ facial expressions to enhance safety. If the 
system detects anger, it may recommend calming actions, such as deep breathing or playing soothing 
music. The software can also identify drowsiness by tracking eye closure, yawning, and blink rate. If the 
system detects that a threshold for these indicators has been reached, it may prompt the driver to pull 
over for safety. 
 
Other indirect methods used to assess someone’s emotional state include eye-tracking, finger pulse, 
speech patterns, and the language they use in online interactions, such as tweets or chat messages. The 
emotional expression level, language choice, and frequency of self-expression on social media can provide 
insights into a person’s mental state, well-being, and personality traits (e.g., extroversion vs. introversion, 
neuroticism vs. calmness). Some companies may use a combination of these methods, such as analyzing 
facial expressions and the language used in online communication, while others may focus on a single 
aspect, like voice tone during phone calls. These indirect emotion-detection techniques help predict 
behaviors, such as determining a person’s job suitability or voting preferences. 

Biometric data is also employed in streaming video games, where spectators watch players, known as 
streamers, play games like Fortnite on popular platforms like Twitch. Some streamers have amassed 
millions of fans, becoming celebrities in their own right. To enhance the viewer experience, tools like "All 
the Feels" have been developed, which overlay biometric and webcam data of a streamer onto the screen 
interface (Robinson et al., 2017). This tool visualizes metrics like heart rate, skin conductance, and 
emotional states through a dashboard, offering an enriched spectator experience and strengthening the
