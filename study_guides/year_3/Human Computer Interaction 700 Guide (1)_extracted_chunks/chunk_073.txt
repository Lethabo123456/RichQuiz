2.2.1 Who to Involve in the Design Process 

There is a surprisingly broad range of people who have a vested interest in the development of a 
successful product, known as stakeholders. Stakeholders are individuals or groups who can either 
influence or be impacted by the success or failure of a project, organization, or product. Stakeholder 
analysis has been a topic of study for many years (Freeman, 1984), and in recent times, there has been 
growing attention on involving stakeholders in interaction design. For instance, Maarten Houben and 
colleagues (2020) conducted workshops with stakeholders to explore requirements for an interactive 
sound cushion intended for use in a dementia care facility. While some of the stakeholders were potential 
users of the cushion, others were not. Their stakeholders included roles like care managers, professional 
caregivers, policymakers, and activity supervisors.

https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/article/what-is-interaction-design  

The group of stakeholders for a specific product is usually broader than the group of actual users. It 
encompasses customers who purchase the product, users who directly interact with it, developers who 
design, build, and maintain it, executives who oversee its profitability, legislators who enforce rules on its 
development and operation, and even people whose lives might be impacted by its launch. The range of 
stakeholders can be quite extensive. A useful way to identify stakeholders is to ask 
who has an interest in the project, who has influence or control over it, and who may be affected by its 
introduction.
