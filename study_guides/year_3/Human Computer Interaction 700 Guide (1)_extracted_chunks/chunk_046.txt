1.5.1 The Components of Interaction Design 
 
Interaction design plays a crucial role across various disciplines and fields that focus on researching and 
designing computer-based systems for people. It intersects with interdisciplinary areas like cognitive 
ergonomics, which combines elements of multiple approaches. While the boundaries between these 
fields can be unclear due to significant overlap, the primary distinctions lie in the methods, philosophies, 
and perspectives they use to study, analyze, and design products. Additionally, the scope and focus of 
each field vary, such as information systems, which applies computing technology in sectors like business, 
health, and education, and ubiquitous computing, which explores the design and deployment of pervasive 
technologies like IoT to enhance social interactions and human experiences. 

1.5.2 Who Is Involved in Interaction Design?  
 
Interaction design involves a wide range of people, from social scientists to filmmakers, reflecting the 
pervasive role of technology in our lives. Designers need to understand not only human behavior and 
communication but also how emotions, aesthetics, desirability, and narrative influence experiences. 
Additionally, they must be aware of business, technical, manufacturing, and marketing considerations, 
along with the ethical challenges, particularly around personal data collection by devices like smart 
speakers and healthcare gadgets. Ensuring new technologies are safe, secure, and trustworthy requires a 
multidisciplinary approach, yet it is rare for a team to include all the necessary professionals, with team 
composition often depending on the project's scope and company goals.
