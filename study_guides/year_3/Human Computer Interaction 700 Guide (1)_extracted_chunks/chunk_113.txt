The Manifesto for Agile Software Development (agilemanifesto.org) underscores values such as 
prioritizing individuals and interactions over processes and tools, working software over comprehensive 
documentation, customer collaboration over contract negotiation, and responding to change over 
following a fixed plan. This approach aligns well with interaction design, particularly in its focus on 
iteration, user feedback, and collaboration with customers. For instance, in Scrum, each sprint typically 
lasts between one and four weeks, with a usable product delivered at the end of each sprint. XP goes even 
further by recommending that the customer be on-site with the developers during the development 
process. 

While the agile approach presents significant advantages for interaction design—such as incorporating 
rapid feedback and close collaboration with customers—integrating agile methods with design practices 
is not always straightforward. Many companies have successfully combined agile with interaction design 
to enhance both user experience and business outcomes, but challenges remain, particularly in 
maintaining effective communication and collaboration across teams with different expertise. This 
complexity is explored further in Chapter 13, which delves into Interaction Design in Practice.
