Chapter 2: THE PROCESS OF INTERACTION DESIGN 
 
2.1 Introduction 
Imagine you're tasked with creating a cloud-based service that allows users to efficiently, safely, and 
enjoyably share and organize various types of content, such as photos, movies, music, chats, and 
documents. How would you approach this? Would you begin by sketching the interface, planning the 
system architecture, or diving straight into coding? Alternatively, would you start by understanding users' 
current experiences with file sharing and examining existing tools like Dropbox and Google Drive? Based 
on this insight, you'd then begin designing the new service. 

This chapter explores the process of interaction design, which focuses on creating interactive products. 
Interaction design involves specific activities aimed at uncovering the product's requirements, designing 
to meet those needs, and producing prototypes for evaluation. The goal is to develop products that 
enhance how people communicate and interact, so engaging various stakeholders in the product 
development process is crucial. This means designing with user-centered concerns at the forefront, rather 
than purely technical ones. 

But how do we determine what kind of product is needed, and how do we identify the right stakeholders? 
Can stakeholders even articulate what they need or want if we simply ask them? Where do interaction 
designers find inspiration for their ideas, and how do they develop designs? Interaction design also 
involves making trade-offs—deciding between conflicting needs, such as how much control the user 
should have versus how much guidance the system should provide. How do you make these decisions? 
Generating alternatives is a key part of the process, but how do you decide which ideas to pursue? 
This chapter addresses these types of questions, explores the principles of people-centered design, and 
introduces the four main activities in the interaction design process. It also presents a lifecycle model of 
interaction design, which outlines these activities and their relationships.
