Design is becoming more participative, with crowdsourcing being a prominent example. Crowdsourcing 
encourages a wide range of people to contribute ideas and examples, bringing different perspectives that 
improve the design, increase satisfaction with the final product, and foster a sense of ownership. 

Another form of large-scale user involvement is citizen engagement, where the goal is to empower a 
community through technology. This approach aims to involve community members in transforming their 
lives, with technology seen as an essential part of the process. For instance, Rajan Vaish and colleagues 
(2017) describe a crowd research initiative that sought to enhance global mobility in the scientific 
community by making research experiences accessible worldwide. Over two years, more than 1,500 
people from 62 countries participated, producing top-tier computer science articles. This example 
demonstrates how community engagement can promote inclusivity and create more opportunities. 
However, involving the general public in design projects can be challenging. Brandon Reynante and 
colleagues (2021) propose a framework for structuring engagement, ensuring inclusive participation, and 
managing large-scale involvement effectively. 

Participatory design, also known as co-creative, cooperative, or co-design, is a design philosophy that 
places end users and other stakeholders at the center of the creation process. Rather than being passive 
recipients of technology, users become active participants in the design of systems, technologies, and 
services. 

The specifics of a project influence how realistic and appropriate user involvement is. If the user group is 
easily identifiable, such as when designing for a specific company, continuous involvement is more 
feasible. However, for products aimed at the general market, it’s unlikely that users will be available to 
join the design team. In such cases, customer experience design, which considers how users interact with 
a brand as a whole, becomes relevant. Box 2.2 (Continued Feedback After Product Feedback)
