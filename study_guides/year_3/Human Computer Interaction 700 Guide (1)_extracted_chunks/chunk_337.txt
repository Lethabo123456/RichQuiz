Rating Scales  

There are several types of rating scales, each serving its own specific purpose (Oppenheim, 2000). Two 
commonly used types are the Likert scale and the semantic differential scale. These scales are designed 
to capture a range of responses to a question, allowing for comparisons across respondents. They are 
particularly useful for obtaining judgments, such as how likely someone is to recommend a product. 
Likert scales involve providing a set of statements that represent a range of possible opinions, while 
semantic differential scales ask respondents to choose between pairs of words that represent differing 
opinions. Likert scales tend to be more commonly used because it is easier to identify statements that 
respondents will consistently understand, whereas semantic pairs can be more difficult to interpret as 
intended. 

Likert Scales   

Likert scales are commonly used to measure opinions, attitudes, and beliefs, making them a popular 
choice for assessing user satisfaction with products. For instance, users' views on the use of color in an 
interface could be evaluated using a Likert scale that employs a range of numbers, as in question 1, or

In both scenarios, respondents would be asked to select one of the boxes, numbers, or phrases. The steps 
for designing a Likert scale are as follows: 

1. Gather a pool of brief statements about the subject being studied. Examples might include, “This 
control panel is clear” and “The procedure for checking credit rating is too complex.” A 
brainstorming session with peers is useful for identifying key areas to explore. 
2. Decide on the scale. Three key questions need to be addressed: How many points should the scale 
have? Should it be discrete or continuous? How should the scale be represented? Refer to Box 8.3 
"What Scale to Use?" for further guidance on this. 
3. Choose items for the final questionnaire and rephrase them if necessary to ensure clarity.
