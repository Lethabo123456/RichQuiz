Efforts also extend to designing accessible technologies in developing regions, addressing global 
disparities. Notably, figures like Aimee Mullins have redefined prosthetics, transforming them from purely 
functional devices into fashionable and desirable accessories. This approach not only challenges the 
boundaries between disabled and nondisabled individuals but also promotes dignity and self-expression, 
demonstrating the transformative power of inclusive design. 

1.9 Usability and User Experience Goals  

Understanding users' needs and goals is a fundamental part of developing interactive products. Designers 
must clarify the primary objective of the product. For example, is it intended to enhance productivity in 
the workplace, serve as a motivational learning tool, or fulfil another purpose? To define these objectives, 
designers classify them into usability goals and user experience goals. 

• Usability goals focus on specific performance criteria, such as efficiency, effectiveness, and ease 
of use. These goals ensure that the product enables users to accomplish tasks with minimal effort 
or errors. 
• User experience goals aim to define the quality of the user's experience, emphasizing aspects like 
aesthetics, emotional satisfaction, and enjoyment. These goals prioritize how the product feels 
and resonates with users beyond its functionality.
