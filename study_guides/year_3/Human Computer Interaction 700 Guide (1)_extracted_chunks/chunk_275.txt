Chapter 7 INTERFACES   
 
7.1 Introduction 
 
The explosion of smartphone app development has led developers to commonly default to designing 
solutions as apps, facilitated by the availability of easy-to-use development tools. In 2022, the app 
industry boasted over 2 million apps on Apple's App Store and over 3 million on Google Play, showcasing 
its scale and popularity. 
However, the web continues to thrive as a platform for offering services, content, and information. A 
significant challenge lies in designing websites that deliver consistent and accessible experiences across 
diverse devices, such as smartwatches, smartphones, laptops, smart TVs, and desktop screens, while 
considering varying form factors. 
Beyond apps and websites, many alternative interfaces have emerged, including voice interfaces, 
gesture-based controls, and multimodal interfaces that combine various input and output modalities. 
Developers now leverage diverse input methods, such as mice, touchpads, pens, joysticks, RFID readers, 
gestures, and even brain-computer interactions. Correspondingly, output technologies have expanded to 
include graphical interfaces, speech, augmented and mixed realities, tangible interfaces, wearable 
devices, and holographic displays. 
This proliferation of interface types has spurred innovative thinking in interaction design and user 
experience (UX). The breadth of available interfaces enables tailored solutions for specific environments, 
users, contexts, and activities. 

The referenced chapter provides a catalogue of 22 interface types, ranging from traditional command-
based interfaces to cutting-edge holographic interfaces. For each type, an overview is presented alongside 
key research and design considerations, offering insights into their applications and usability. Some 
interfaces, being more established, are discussed in greater detail, while others are briefly introduced, 
reflecting the evolving nature of interaction design.
