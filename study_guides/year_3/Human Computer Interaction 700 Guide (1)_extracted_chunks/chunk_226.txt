5.5.3 Shareable Interfaces   
 
Various technologies have been developed to enhance coordination and awareness in collaborative 
environments. These include tools like whiteboards, large touchscreens, and multitouch tables, which 
allow multiple people to interact with digital content simultaneously. Early research, such as the work by 
Müller-Tomfelde (2010), explored how shared technologies could improve group collaboration, especially 
when co-located individuals work together. The hypothesis was that shared interfaces offer greater 
flexibility for collaboration compared to single-user interfaces, enabling users to engage with the content 
in real time. 

The use of touch-based inputs, such as fingers or pens on shared surfaces, makes interactions observable 
by others, increasing situational and peripheral awareness. This visibility allows participants to be more 
attuned to each other's actions and intentions, fostering better coordination. Additionally, shared 
surfaces are seen as more intuitive and less intimidating than other technologies, encouraging users to 
interact freely without feeling self-conscious about their actions. For instance, research showed that small 
groups found it easier and more comfortable to work around a tabletop display than to sit in front of a PC 
or stand in front of a vertical display (Rogers and Lindley, 2004), highlighting the naturalness of 
collaborative, touch-enabled technologies.
