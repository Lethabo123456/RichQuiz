7.2.2 Graphical User Interfaces 
 
The Xerox Star interface marked a ground breaking moment in the history of interaction design by 
introducing the Graphical User Interface (GUI). This innovation revolutionized the way users interacted 
with computer systems, shifting from text-based commands to a visually intuitive and engaging interface. 
Here's an overview of the Xerox Star's influence and the WIMP paradigm: 
 
Key Components of the WIMP Interface: 

1. Windows: 
o Multiple sections of the screen could display different content or applications 
simultaneously. 
o Users could manipulate windows by scrolling, resizing, overlapping, opening, closing, and 
moving them using a pointing device. 

2. Icons: 
o Small, graphical representations of applications, commands, or tools. 
o Clicking on an icon would open or activate the associated application or function. 
3. Menus: 
o Lists of selectable options, similar to a restaurant menu. 
o Provided an organized way to access commands and settings. 

4. Pointing Device: 
o The mouse was introduced as a pointing device, allowing users to move a cursor to interact 
with windows, menus, and icons on the screen. 
 
Early WIMP GUI Characteristics: 
• Boxy Design: 
o Early interfaces were primarily rectangular and relied heavily on widgets like checkboxes, 
dialog boxes, scrollbars, panels, and palettes. 
o The design was functional but limited by the widgets available at the time. 

• Widgets: 
o Standardized visual elements (e.g., buttons, scrollbars) that users could manipulate to 
perform tasks. 
o Dialog boxes, a prominent widget, were widely used for user input and system feedback. 
 
Evolution of GUIs: 
1. From WIMP to Touch-Based Interfaces: 
o The GUI paradigm has been adapted to new platforms, such as smartphones and tablets. 
o Interaction now involves swiping, pinching, and tapping, replacing the mouse and 
keyboard as primary input methods. 
2. Modern Aesthetic: 
o Current GUIs emphasize flat or material design principles with a focus on minimalism, 
responsiveness, and accessibility. 
o Features like fluid animations, customizable widgets, and gesture-based interactions 
dominate. 
3. Broader Device Integration: 
o GUIs have extended beyond desktops to include devices like smart TVs, wearables, and 
automotive displays.
