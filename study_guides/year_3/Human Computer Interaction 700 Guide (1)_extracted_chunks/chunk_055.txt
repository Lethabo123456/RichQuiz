1.8 Accessibility and Inclusiveness 
 
Accessibility and inclusivity are fundamental principles in interaction design, aiming to ensure interactive 
products are usable by as many people as possible, including those with disabilities. Major companies like 
Google and Apple provide tools to support accessibility, such as Android's screen readers and Apple’s 
Voice Over, which narrates device interactions and identifies elements in photos. Inclusiveness goes 
further, striving to accommodate people of varying ages, disabilities, and socioeconomic backgrounds, 
ensuring equal access to products and services. 

The nature of disability and its impact can change over time, influenced by recovery, environmental 
conditions, or even temporary circumstances like being in a noisy environment. Poor interaction design 
often creates barriers, rather than the impairment itself. Accessibility opens up experiences for all, often 
driving innovations that later become mainstream. For example, SMS was originally developed for 
hearing-impaired users. 

Designing for accessibility involves two approaches: inclusive design and assistive technology. Inclusive 
design aims to eliminate barriers from the start, such as choosing colour schemes that are distinguishable 
for everyone, including those with colour blindness. Assistive technologies, on the other hand, are tailored 
solutions for specific impairments, such as screen readers for vision loss or communication aids for 
physical disabilities. Disabilities can be permanent, temporary, or situational, requiring varied design 
considerations. 

As people age, their functional abilities often diminish. For instance, older adults may struggle to hear in 
noisy environments. Many with permanent disabilities consider assistive technologies, like wheelchairs 
or communication aids, integral to their identity and independence. Current research in Human-
Computer Interaction (HCI) explores how IoT, wearables, and virtual reality can enhance assistive 
technologies. Disability interactions (DIX) offer a cross-disciplinary approach to co-create technologies 
with disabled individuals, fostering innovation and empowerment.
