8.4.2 Structured Interviews   
In structured interviews, the interviewer uses predetermined questions, similar to those found in a 
questionnaire, ensuring that each participant is asked the same set of questions to maintain 
standardization. These questions are typically short, clearly worded, and often closed-ended, meaning 
the answers come from a predefined set of options (with an “other” option, though ideally used 
sparingly). Closed questions are effective when the range of possible responses is known or when 
participants are short on time. Structured interviews are most useful when the study’s goals are well-
defined, and specific questions can be pinpointed.
 
Example structured interview questions include: 
• “Which of the following apps do you use most frequently: Prime Video, GoogleTV, or Netflix?” 
• “How often do you watch streamed content: every day, once a week, once a month, or less than 
once a month?” 
• “Do you ever purchase anything online: Yes/No? If Yes, how often: every day, once a week, once 
a month, or less frequently than once a month?” 
The questions are asked in the same order for each participant, ensuring consistency throughout the 
interview.
