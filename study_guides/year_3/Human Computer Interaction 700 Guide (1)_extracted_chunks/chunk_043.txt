The Internet of Things (IoT) has transformed everyday household products by enabling devices to collect 
data from sensors and communicate over the internet. A common example is home security systems, 
which allow homeowners to monitor their property through data relayed to their smartphones. These 
systems often include features like motion detectors, glass break sensors, and smart object detectors. 
Video doorbells, for instance, send live footage to a smartphone app, enabling homeowners to see who 
is at the door, even while they are away. Advanced cameras also integrate machine learning to distinguish 
between familiar faces and potential intruders, enhancing security measures. 
 
Machine learning extends beyond security systems to other IoT-enabled household products, such as 
automated thermostats like Nest. These devices analyze energy consumption patterns to optimize 
temperature settings, improving energy efficiency. However, interaction design faces critical challenges 
in ensuring that these systems are not only effective and user-friendly but also maintain the safety and 
privacy of the data they collect. Designers must make informed decisions that balance usability with 
protecting personal information, addressing the growing concerns of privacy and security in connected 
homes.
