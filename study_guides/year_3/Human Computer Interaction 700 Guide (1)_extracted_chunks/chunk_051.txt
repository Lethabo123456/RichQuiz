Designing interactive products involves considering various aspects of user experience, such as usability, 
functionality, aesthetics, content, look and feel, and emotional appeal. Jack Carroll (2004) highlights 
additional factors, including fun, health, social capital, and cultural identity shaped by characteristics like 
age, ethnicity, and occupation. Researchers like Kasper Hornbæk and Morten Hertzum (2017) emphasize 
how users perceive and emotionally react to products, such as seeing a smartwatch as sleek or feeling 
positive while using it. Similarly, Marc Hassenzahl and colleagues have noted an increased focus on 
hedonic design elements, which evoke and stimulate user interactions to enhance well-being. 
John McCarthy and Peter Wright (2004) explore the role of expectations and the sense-making process in 
user experiences, offering the Technology as Experience framework to examine how technology feels to 
its users. Extending this concept, Kia Höök (2018) introduced Soma Design, which considers how 
technology can heighten awareness of bodily sensations and movements. While there is no universal 
formula for creating quality user experiences, interaction designers can rely on conceptual frameworks, 
proven design methods, guidelines, and research findings to guide their efforts. These tools and insights 
provide a foundation for crafting meaningful and engaging experiences.
