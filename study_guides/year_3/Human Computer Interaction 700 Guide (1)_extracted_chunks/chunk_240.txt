Chapter 6: EMOTIONAL INTERACTION  
  
6.1 Introduction 
  
How do you react to bad news? Do you feel upset, sad, angry, annoyed, or perhaps a combination of these 
emotions? Does it affect your mood for the entire day? Now, imagine a wearable device capable of 
detecting your emotions and offering suggestions to improve your mood, especially on particularly tough 
days. Would you find such a device helpful, or would the idea of a machine attempting to cheer you up 
feel unsettling? 
The field of emotional AI, also known as affective computing, focuses on developing technology that can 
automatically recognize emotions by analysing facial expressions, body movements, gestures, and other 
cues. This research has numerous applications beyond mood improvement, such as health, retail, driving, 
and education. For instance, these systems could identify emotions like happiness, frustration, or 
boredom and trigger appropriate interventions, such as suggesting a moment of reflection or 
recommending specific activities. 
Another related area is emotional design, which involves creating technologies that elicit specific 
emotional states. Examples include apps that encourage users to reflect on their moods and feelings or 

technologies like virtual pets that foster emotional attachment. Emotional design also explores how social 
robots might reduce loneliness and how emotive feedback can influence behaviour. 
This chapter explores emotional interaction—a term encompassing both emotional design and affective 
computing. It begins by examining emotions and their role in shaping behaviour and everyday 
experiences. The chapter then delves into how an interface’s design impacts usability and user 
experience, with a focus on expressive and persuasive interfaces that influence emotions and behaviours. 
It also discusses technologies that detect human emotions through voice and facial recognition, and the 
role of anthropomorphism in interaction design.
