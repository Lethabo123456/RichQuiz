While the distinction between usability and user experience goals provides clarity, it is essential to view 
them as interconnected. Usability often underpins the overall quality of the user experience; for example, 
a poorly designed interface can detract from an otherwise visually appealing product. Similarly, aesthetic 
and emotional factors can influence perceived usability.
 
Historically, Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) research emphasized usability. However, the field has 
evolved to encompass a broader spectrum of user experience considerations. Today's designers strive to 
integrate usability with experiential qualities, creating products that are not only functional but also 
engaging, enjoyable, and meaningful.
