8.7.1 Choosing Techniques  
 
Choosing the right data gathering techniques depends on various factors related to the study's objectives. 
Table 8.1 provides a helpful overview for selecting a set of techniques based on the specific project needs. 
It includes the type of information gathered (e.g., answers to specific questions), the data type (qualitative 
or quantitative), as well as the advantages and disadvantages of each technique. Additionally, some 
techniques can be conducted in different modalities, such as face-to-face, by phone, or through 
teleconferencing, which should be considered when evaluating the pros and cons of each method. 

When choosing a data gathering technique, practical considerations play a crucial role. These include: 
• The focus of the study: The data needed should align with the study's focus and goals, which will 
depend on the interaction design activity and the stage of the design process. 
• The participants involved: Characteristics of the participant group, such as their location, and 
whether the data collection will happen remotely, influence the choice of technique. 
• The nature of the technique: Some techniques may require specialist equipment or training, and 
it's important to ensure the investigators have the necessary expertise. 
• Available resources: This includes factors like expertise, access to participants, tool support, time, 
and budget, all of which affect the feasibility of certain techniques.
