6.4 Affective Computing and Emotional AI 
Affective computing, a term introduced by Rosalind Picard in 1997, refers to the use of computers to 
recognize and express emotions in a way similar to humans. This involves developing techniques to assess 
emotions such as frustration, stress, and mood by analysing people's facial expressions and conversations, 
as well as creating wearable sensors that allow individuals to communicate their emotional states. More 
recently, affective computing has expanded to explore how emotions impact personal health (Jacques et 
al., 2017). Another goal is to design computers and robots capable of appropriately responding to human 
emotions and moods, knowing when and how to show empathy (Schuller et al., 2021). 
Specifically, emotional AI aims to automate the measurement of emotions and behaviors using AI 
technologies that analyse facial expressions and voice to infer emotional states. 

6.4.1 Measuring and Tracking Affect and Emotions   
 
Various sensing technologies are employed in affective computing and emotional AI to measure and track 
physiological processes, using the collected data to predict aspects of a person's behavior. For instance, 
these systems can forecast what someone is likely to purchase online based on their emotional state, 
such as feeling sad, bored, or happy. The primary techniques and technologies used for this include: 

• Cameras to measure facial expressions 
• Biosensors on fingers or palms to assess galvanic skin response, which indicates anxiety or 
nervousness through sweat 
• Analyzing affective expressions in speech, including voice quality, pitch, intonation, loudness, and 
rhythm 
• Body movement and gestures, detected by motion capture systems or accelerometer sensors 
placed on the body
