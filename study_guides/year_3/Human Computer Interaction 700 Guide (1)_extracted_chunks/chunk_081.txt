2.2.3 Degrees of User Involvement 
 
User involvement in the design process can vary greatly, from full engagement throughout every stage of 
development to more targeted participation in specific activities. It can also range from small groups of 
users in face-to-face settings to large numbers of users contributing online. Occasionally, users may be 
integrated into the design team, becoming major contributors to the product’s development. However, 
this full-time involvement can make them less connected to their original community and context, while 
part-time involvement may create a heavy workload. On the positive side, having users actively involved 
in the team, whether full-time or part-time, ensures continuous input. 

More commonly, users participate in specific activities that help inform development or evaluate designs 
once available. When user involvement is limited, methods like personas can be used to keep users' 
concerns at the forefront of the design and development process. 

User participation can take place in small face-to-face sessions for information gathering or design 
evaluation, but increasingly, online sessions allow many thousands of users to contribute. While face-to-
face involvement and in-situ studies still have value, the range of possibilities for user engagement has 
expanded. One example of this is online feedback exchange (OFE) systems, which allow millions of 
potential users to test design concepts before they are released to the market.
