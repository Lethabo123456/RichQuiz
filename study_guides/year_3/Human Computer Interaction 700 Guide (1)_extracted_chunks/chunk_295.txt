8.2 Six Key Issues  
 
Six key issues require attention for any data gathering session to be successful: goal setting, identifying p
articipants, the relationship between the data collector and the data provider, ethical considerations of 
collecting data, triangulation, and pilot studies.   
 
8.2.1 Setting Goals   
 
The primary purpose of gathering data is to gain insights into people, their behaviours, or their 
interactions with technology. For instance, it could involve studying how technology integrates into family 
life, determining which of two "upload file" icons is more intuitive, or assessing whether a redesigned 
smart meter is more memorable than its predecessor. The reasons for collecting data are diverse, and 
clearly defining the study's goals is essential. These goals shape the design of data collection sessions, the 
techniques employed, and the analysis conducted (Robson and McCartan, 2016). 
Goals can range from informal to highly formal. In online experiments like A/B testing, goals are often underpinned 
by mathematically defined metrics, aimed at evaluating two or more design alternatives. Combining multiple metrics 
into a single evaluation criterion can be challenging, as noted by Ron Kohaviet al. (2020). Consequently, multiple 
metric values are often used to define the goal. For example, Figure 8.1 illustrates one of the organizational metrics 

employed to monitor the performance of Bing’s search engine. 
 
A less formal approach to defining study goals may be adequate for testing initial concepts or conducting 
exploratory research. For instance, Abir Ghorayeb et al. (2021) conducted a study aimed at understanding 
older adults' perceptions of smart homes and how their experiences shaped those views. 
Regardless of the format, data-gathering goals should be well-defined enough to clearly determine when 
they have been achieved. The criteria for recognizing goal achievement will depend on the specific 
techniques employed.
