(Rogers, Sharp and Preece, 2023) 
Many physical transactions have been transformed into digital ones in recent years. Examples include 
purchasing tickets for entertainment events like movies, concerts, or plays, as well as booking travel 
tickets for trains, buses, or flights. One of the main advantages of digitalizing these transactions is the 
elimination of long lines at ticket counters, allowing customers to select and purchase tickets from the 
comfort of their homes. Additionally, digital platforms allow users to save and access preferences for 
tickets, and provide convenient QR codes or barcodes that can be scanned using a smartphone or watch 
for easy entry. Online booking also offers the option to choose seating and even order food or drinks in 
advance, while digital tickets can be stored in digital wallets for easy future reference. 
However, there are some drawbacks to digital ticketing. For one, it requires access to a smartphone 
capable of downloading and storing tickets, which excludes those with older phones or people who prefer 

paper tickets. Additionally, the process can be stressful for those with low phone battery or vision 
difficulties. While printing out digital tickets is an option, it assumes the user has access to a printer. For 
individuals with discounts, such as students or seniors, showing a discount card in person can be 
inconvenient, especially when switching between apps. Furthermore, some people, particularly those 
with disabilities or using assistive technologies, may face difficulties using digital tickets, which could lead 
to legal or ethical issues. Finally, some users may prefer to remain anonymous and avoid sharing personal 
information online, preferring to pay in cash instead.
