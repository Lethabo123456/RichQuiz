2.2 What Is Involved in Interaction Design?  
 
Design exists across various fields, such as graphic design, architectural design, industrial design, and 
software design, with each having its own unique approach. However, there are common principles 
shared across these disciplines. The Design Council in the United Kingdom captures these similarities 
through the "double diamond" design process, as shown in Figure 2.1. This approach is divided into four 
phases, which are split into two parts. The left side of each diamond represents divergent thinking 
(broadly exploring the problem), while the right side focuses on convergent thinking (narrowing down to 
a specific solution). 
https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/interaction-design-process  

The four phases are: 
1. Discover: Designers start by understanding the problem without making assumptions, engaging 
with people affected by the issue. 
2. Define: Building on insights gained from the Discover phase, designers redefine the challenge in a 
clearer way. 
3. Develop: A variety of solutions are generated, inspired by competing products, insights from 
earlier phases, and brainstorming. These solutions are co-designed with a diverse group of people. 
4. Deliver: The proposed solutions are tested on a small scale, refined, or rejected as necessary to 
evolve into better solutions. 

As shown in the diagram, this process is not strictly linear; the phases may be revisited and iterated
multiple times as designers move from identifying the challenge to reaching the final outcome. As the
Design Council notes, "in a constantly changing and digital world, no idea is ever 'finished.'"

The double diamond process is supported by design principles, a set of methods, and two key cultural
aspects within organizations: engagement and leadership. The four key design principles that underpin
the process focus on people, communication, collaboration, and iteration. These principles align closely
with the core values of interaction design. Many of the techniques in the methods supporting the double
diamond are also covered in this book. A people-centered design approach emphasizes the importance
of involving different stakeholders and fostering conditions conducive to innovation. Importantly, the
double diamond approach is relevant not only for new products but also for evolving existing ones and
addressing new challenges.
