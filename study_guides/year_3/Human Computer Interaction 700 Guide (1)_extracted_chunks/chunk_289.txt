7.4 Which Interface?  
This chapter provided an overview of the wide range of interfaces currently available or under 
development, illustrating the evolution from the command-based systems of the 1970s to the diverse 
options we have today. This progress raises an important question: which interface should be chosen, and 
how should it be designed? The answer largely depends on the specific requirements and opportunities 
for the intended user experience. 
For instance, if designing a healthcare app to help patients monitor their dietary intake, a mobile interface 
equipped with capabilities like barcode scanning or food item recognition through machine learning 
would be ideal. Such an interface combines mobility, object recognition, and ease of use. Conversely, 
designing a collaborative work environment for co-located group decision-making might benefit from 
integrating shareable technologies, conversational agents, and personal devices that facilitate seamless 
transitions between individual and group activities. 

Deciding on the most suitable interface for a given task involves addressing several questions: Is 
multimedia more effective than tangible interfaces for learning? How does voice function as a command-
based interface? Are multimodal interfaces superior to single-media ones? Are wearable interfaces more 
effective than mobile devices for navigating foreign cities? What distinguishes VR from AR, and which is 
better suited for gaming? Are tangible environments more immersive and engaging than virtual worlds? 
The selection of an interface depends on multiple factors, including the type of task, the intended users, 
the context of use, and practical considerations such as reliability, social acceptability, privacy, ethics, and 
location. Ultimately, the appropriateness, utility, efficiency, engagement, and supportiveness of an 
interface are determined by how these factors interact within a given scenario.
