3.5.3 Zero–One Matrices 
A matrix all of whose entries are either 0 or 1 is called a zero–one matrix. 
Zero–one matrices are often used to represent discrete structures. 
Algorithms using these structures are based on Boolean arithmetic with 
zero–one matrices. This arithmetic is based on the Boolean operations ∧ and 
∨, which operate on pairs of bits, defined by: 
 
 
Definition 46 
Let A = [aij ] and B = [bij ] be m × n zero–one matrices. Then the join of A and 
B is the zero–one matrix with (i, j )th entry aij ∨ bij . The join of A and B is 
denoted by A ∨ B. The meet of A and B is the zero–one matrix with (i, j )th 
entry aij ∧ bij . The meet of A and B is denoted by A ∧ B.
