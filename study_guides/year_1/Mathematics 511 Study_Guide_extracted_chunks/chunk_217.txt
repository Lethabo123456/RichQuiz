Worked Example 
What are the degrees and what are the neighborhoods of the vertices in 
the graphs G and H displayed below. 
 
Solution:  
In G, deg(a) = 2, deg(b) = deg(c) = deg(f ) = 4, deg(d ) = 1, deg(e) = 3, and 
deg(g) = 0.  
The neighborhoods of these vertices are N(a) = {b, f }, N(b) = {a, c, e, f }, 
N(c) = {b, d, e, f }, N(d) = {c}, N(e) = {b, c, f }, N(f ) = {a, b, c, e}, and N(g) = 
∅. In H, deg(a) = 4, deg(b) = deg(e) = 6, deg(c) = 1, and deg(d ) = 5. 
The neighborhoods of these vertices are N(a) = {b, d, e}, N(b) = {a, b, c, d, 
e}, N(c) = {b}, N(d) = {a, b, e}, and N(e) = {a, b, d}. 

A vertex of degree zero is called isolated. It follows that an isolated vertex is 
not adjacent to any vertex. Vertex g in graph G in the example above is 
isolated. A vertex is pendant if and only if it has degree one. Consequently, a 
pendant vertex is adjacent to exactly one other vertex. Vertex d in graph G 
in example above is pendant. 
 
Theorem  
The Handshaking Theorem: Let G = (V ,E) be an undirected graph with m 
edges. Then 
 
2𝑚 = ∑𝑣∈𝐴 deg (𝑣).
 
(Note that this applies even if multiple edges and loops are present.) 
 
Imagine we want to know how many edges are there in a graph with 10 
vertices each of degree six.
Because the sum of the degrees of the vertices is 6⋅10 = 60, it follows that 
2m = 60 where m is the number of edges. Therefore, m = 30. 
 
Theorem  
An undirected graph has an even number of vertices of odd degree.
