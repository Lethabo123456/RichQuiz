A computer network may contain multiple links between data centers, as 
shown in Figure 4.1. To model such networks we need graphs that have more 
than one edge connecting the same pair of vertices. Graphs that may have 
multiple edges connecting the same vertices are called multigraphs. When 
there are m different edges associated to the same unordered pair of 
vertices {u, v}, we also say that {u, v} is an edge of multiplicity m
different copies of an edge {u, v}. 
 
Figure 4.2: A Computer Network with Multiple Links between Data Centers. 
 
Sometimes a communications link connects a data center with itself, perhaps 
a feedback loop for diagnostic purposes. Such a network is illustrated in 
Figure 4.3. To model this network we need to include edges that connect a 
vertex to itself. Such edges are called loops, and sometimes we may even 
have more than one loop at a vertex. Graphs that may include loops, and 
possibly multiple edges connecting the same pair of vertices or a vertex to 
itself, are sometimes called pseudographs. 
 
Figure 4.3: A Computer Network with Diagnostic Links. 
 
So far the graphs we have introduced are undirected graphs. Their edges are 
also said to be undirected. However, to construct a graph model, we may 
find it necessary to assign directions to the edges of a graph. For example, in 
a computer network, some links may operate in only one direction (such 
links are called single duplex lines). This may be the case if there is a large 
amount of traffic sent to some data centers, with little or no traffic going in 
the opposite direction. Such a network is shown in Figure 4.4. 
To model such a computer network we use a directed graph. Each edge of a 
directed graph is associated to an ordered pair.
