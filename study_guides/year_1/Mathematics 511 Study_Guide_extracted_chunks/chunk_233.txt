4.3.4 Isomorphism of Graphs 
We often need to know whether it is possible to draw two graphs in the same 
way. That is, do the graphs have the same structure when we ignore the 
identities of their vertices? For instance, in chemistry, graphs are used to 
model chemical compounds. 
Different compounds can have the same molecular formula but can differ in 
structure. Such compounds can be represented by graphs that cannot be 
drawn in the same way. The graphs representing previously known 
compounds can be used to determine whether a supposedly new compound 
has been studied before.
