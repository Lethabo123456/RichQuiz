1.1.2 Conditional Statements 
 
Definition 5 
Let 𝑝 and 𝑞 be propositions. The conditional statement 𝑝 → 𝑞 is the 
proposition “if 𝑝, then 𝑞.” The conditional statement 𝑝 → 𝑞 is false when 𝑝 
is true and 𝑞 is false, and true otherwise. In the conditional statement 𝑝 →
 𝑞, 𝑝 is called the hypothesis (or antecedent or premise) and 𝑞 is called the 
conclusion (or consequence). 
 
The statement 𝑝 → 𝑞 is called a conditional statement because 𝑝 → 𝑞 
asserts that 𝑞 is true on the condition that 𝑝 holds. A conditional statement 
is also called an implication. The truth table for the conditional statement 
𝑝 → 𝑞 is shown in Table 5. Note that the statement 𝑝 → 𝑞 is true when 
both 𝑝 and 𝑞 are true and when 𝑝 is false (no matter what truth value 𝑞 has).
