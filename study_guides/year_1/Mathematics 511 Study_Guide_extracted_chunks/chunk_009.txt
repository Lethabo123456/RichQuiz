1.1.1 Propositions 
A proposition is a directive statement that is either true or false, but not 
both. The following are some examples of propositions: 

 It is not a holiday today. 
 It is raining today. 
 The attendance of students in the class is low today 
 Washington, D.C., is the capital of the United States of America. 
 1 +  1 =  2. 
 2 +  2 =  3. 
 India’s prime minister during the years 1971-1980 was a lady. 
 There exists an integer 𝑛 such that 2𝑛+ 4 = 5 
 
LEARNING OUTCOMES 
 
1. Demonstrate ability to formulate and interpret statements presented in 
Boolean logic; 
2. Demonstrate an understanding of how to reformulate statements from 
common language to formal logic and apply truth tables and the rules of 
propositional and predicate calculus; 
3. Demonstrate the ability to formulate short proofs using the following 
methods: Direct proof, Indirect proof, Proof by contradiction, and Case 
analysis.
