1.4.1 Predicates

“𝑥 >  3, ” “𝑥 =  𝑦 +  3, ” “𝑥 +  𝑦 =  𝑧, ” 
and 
“computer 𝑥 is under attack by an intruder,” 
and 
“computer 𝑥 is functioning properly,” 

are often found in mathematical assertions, in computer programs, and in 
system specifications. These statements are neither true nor false when the 
values of the variables are not specified. In this section, we will discuss the 
ways that propositions can be produced from such statements. The 
statement “𝑥 is greater than 3” has two parts. The first part, the variable 𝑥, 
is the subject of the statement. The second part—the predicate, “is greater 
than 3”—refers to a property that the subject of the statement can have.We 
can denote the statement “𝑥 is greater than 3” by 𝑃(𝑥), where P denotes 
the predicate “is greater than 3” and 𝑥 is the variable. The statement 𝑃(𝑥) 
is also said to be the value of the propositional function P at 𝑥. Once a value
