2.1.2 Semicolons 
Simple statements in JavaScript are generally followed by a semicolon character, just 
as they are in C, C++, and Java. JavaScript, however, allows you to omit this semicolon 
if your statements are each placed on a separate line. It is a good programming 
practice to use semicolons. 
 
2.1.3 Case Sensitivity 
JavaScript is a case-sensitive language. This means that language keywords, variables, 
function names, and any other identifiers must always be typed with a consistent 
capitalization of letters. So identifiers Time, TIme and TIME will have different 
meanings in JavaScript. Care should be taken while writing your variable and function 
names in JavaScript.
