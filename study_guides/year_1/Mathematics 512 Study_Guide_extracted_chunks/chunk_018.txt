2.4 Solving Quadratic Equations using Graphs 
In this section we will see how graphs can be used to solve quadratic 
equations. If the coefficient of x2 in the quadratic expression  ax2 + bx + c is 
positive then a graph of y = ax2 + bx + c will take the form shown in                  
Figure 2.4(a) If the coefficient of x2 is negative the graph will take the form 
shown in Figure 2.4(b) 
  
 
Figure 2.4(a) 
 
Figure 2.4(b) 
 
We will now add x and y axes. Figure 2.4(c) shows what can happen when we 
plot a graph of y = ax2 + bx + c for the case in which a is positive. 
Figure 2.4(c) 
The horizontal line, the x axis, corresponds to points on the graph where y = 0. 
So points where the graph touches or crosses this axis correspond to solutions 
of ax2 + bx + c = 0. 
In Figure 2.4(c), the graph in (a) never cuts or touches the horizontal axis and 
so this corresponds to a quadratic equation ax2 + bx + c = 0 having no real 
roots.
