3.1 Tangents and Limits  
A tangent to a curve is a straight line that touches the curve at a single point 
but does not intersect it at that point. For example, in the figure to the right, 
the y-axis would not be considered a tangent line because it intersects the 
curve at the origin. A secant to a curve is a straight line that intersects the 
curve at two or more points.
