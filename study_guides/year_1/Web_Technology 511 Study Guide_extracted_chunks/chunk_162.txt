Semantic elements are elements with a meaning. They clearly describe their meaning 
to the browser and the developer. 

The header element     <header> 
The header element is used to contain the content that appears at the top of every 
page of your website: the logo, tagline, search prompt, and possibly a navigational 
menu. The header is commonly placed at the top of a page as it contains the 
introductory content or navigational aids that are specific to a page or website. 
However the position of a header is not constrained to the top of the page,  a header 
can also be used to introduce each section of your content. For example, <div 
id="header"> 
 
The section Element   <section> 
The section element represents a generic section of a document or application. The 
<section> element is used to group standalone sections within a webpage containing 
logically connected content (news block, contact information, etc). A section, in this 
context, is a thematic grouping of content, typically with a heading. A section is used as 
a generic container and not for styling and scripting purposes. 
Some examples of acceptable uses for section elements include: 

 individual sections of a tabbed interface 
 segments of an “About” page; for example, a company’s “About” page might 
include sections on the company’s history, its mission statement, and its 
team 
 different parts of a lengthy “terms of service” page 
 various sections of an online news site; for example, articles could be 
grouped into sections covering sports, world affairs, and economic news.
