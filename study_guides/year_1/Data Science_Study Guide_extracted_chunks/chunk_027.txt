1.4 Fundamental Terminology 
Prior to venturing forward, it would be astute to understand a few fundamental terms related to the 
field of Data Science. 
 
• Machine learning: Refers to granting computers, the capacity to learn from data without explicit 
"rules" provided by a programmer is referred to as machine learning. Machine learning automates 
the identification of links in data and the construction of robust data models by fusing the 
computing power of computers with intelligent learning algorithms. In relation to data models, 
we shall focus on the following two fundamental types of data models (Burns, 2023): 
• Probabilistic model: This refers to using probability to find a relationship between 
elements that includes a degree of randomness. 
• Statistical model: This refers to taking advantage of statistical theorems to formalize 
relationships between data elements in a (usually) simple mathematical formula. 
• Data Mining refers to the process of discovering valuable and formerly unknown patterns, 
relationships, or insights from large sets of data. It involves employing various statistical and 
computational techniques to analyse data, identify trends, and extract meaningful information 
that can be used for decision-making and prediction.  
• Exploratory data analysis (EDA) refers to preparing data in order to standardize results and gain 
insights. EDA is concerned with data visualization and preparation. This is where we turn 
unorganized data into organized data and also clean up missing/incorrect data points. During EDA, 
we will create many types of plots and use these plots to identify key features and relationships to 
exploit in our data models (Sinan, 2016).
