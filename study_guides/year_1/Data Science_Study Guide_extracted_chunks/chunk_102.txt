Fundamentally, we observe numerous instances of the event and count the number of times A was 
satisfied. The division of these numbers is an approximation of the probability (Sinan, 2016). 
 
The Bayesian approach differs by dictating that probabilities must be distinguished using theoretical 
means. Using the Bayes approach, we would have to think a bit more critically about events and why 
they occur. Neither methodology is 100% the correct answer all the time. Typically, it comes down to 
the problem and the difficulty of using either method. The core of the Frequentist approach is the 
relative frequency (Sinan, 2016). The relative frequency of an event is how often an event occurs 
divided by the total number of observations.
