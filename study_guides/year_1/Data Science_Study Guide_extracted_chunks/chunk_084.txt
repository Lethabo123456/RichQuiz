It can be thought that reinforcement is like supervised learning in that the agent is learning from its 
past actions to make better moves in the future; nevertheless, the main difference lies in the reward. 
The reward does not have to be tied in any way to a "correct" or "incorrect" decision. The reward 
simply encourages (or discourages) different actions (Sinan, 2016). 
 
Reinforcement learning is the least explored of the three types of machine learning and therefore is 
not explored in great length in this text. The remainder of the chapter will focus on supervised and 
unsupervised learning. 
 
Advantages of Supervised Machine Learning: 
• Predictive Power: Supervised learning algorithms can make accurate predictions on new, 
unseen data once they are trained on labelled data. This is especially useful in tasks like 
classification and regression. 
• Feedback Mechanism: With labelled data, the algorithm receives feedback during training, 
enabling it to learn from its mistakes and improve its performance iteratively. 
• Interpretable Results: In many cases, the trained models are interpretable, allowing users to 
understand the factors influencing predictions. 
• Well-studied Techniques: Supervised learning has been extensively studied and applied in 
various domains, resulting in a wide range of robust algorithms and tools. 
• Wide Applicability: Supervised learning can be applied to both structured and unstructured 
data, making it versatile for various problem domains (Valcheva, 2022). 
 
Disadvantages of Supervised Machine Learning: 
• Data Dependency: Supervised learning requires labelled data for training, which can be 
expensive and time-consuming to obtain, especially for large-scale datasets. 
• Overfitting: If the model is too complex or the dataset is too small, it may memorize the 
training data and perform poorly on new, unseen data. 
• Bias and Noise: The quality of predictions heavily depends on the quality and 
representativeness of the training data. Biased or noisy data can lead to biased models.
• Limited Generalization: Models trained on specific data may not generalize well to different 
distributions or real-world scenarios not seen during training (Valcheva, 2022).
